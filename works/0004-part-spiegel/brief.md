# Brief: "Spiegel im Spiegel" -- Arvo Part (1978)

## Work

**Title:** Spiegel im Spiegel (Mirror in the Mirror)
**Composer:** Arvo Part
**Composed:** 1978
**Published:** 1978 (Universal Edition)
**Duration:** approximately 10 minutes
**Tradition:** minimalism / tintinnabuli / sacred music

## Instrumentation

Piano and violin (original version). Also widely performed with cello replacing violin.

## Structure

No sectional divisions. The piece unfolds in a single arc:

- Piano: arpeggiated A major triad in 6/4 throughout, ascending and descending, constant rhythmic pulse
- Violin/cello: single sustained pitches, each held for multiple beats, moving stepwise through the A major scale (occasionally with repeated pitches for extended duration)

The violin's pitch movement is extremely slow: each note of the scale may be held for 20-30+ seconds before moving to the adjacent pitch. The combined effect is a texture of extreme stasis in which the piano's constant arpeggiation defines the harmonic field while the violin's sustained pitch floats within it.

**Tintinnabuli method:** Two voices in strict relationship. The melodic voice (violin) moves stepwise through the diatonic scale. The tintinnabulus voice (piano) arpegiates the tonic triad without developing, modulating, or departing from its fixed pattern. The name means "little bells" -- the arpeggiated triad rings like a bell throughout the piece.

## Tradition Context

"Spiegel im Spiegel" was composed in 1978, two years after Part emerged from his eight-year compositional silence with "Fur Alina" (1976), the first tintinnabuli work. Part had spent those eight years studying medieval polyphony, Gregorian chant, and early Renaissance music, arriving at a compositional method of extreme austerity.

The piece is not minimalism in the American sense (Reich, Glass, Riley) -- it is not process music and it is not about audible transformation over time. It is the application of the tintinnabuli method to a specific image: a mirror reflecting a mirror, each reflection containing all previous reflections, the image infinite within a finite space.

## Key Questions for Panel

1. Is the tintinnabuli method "structure" in the rubric's sense? Does the mirror logic constitute architecture?
2. How does the Economy dimension handle a piece where the primary unit is not a note but a sustained tone held for many beats -- and where the most economical choice is to use almost nothing?
3. What is Beethoven's response to a piece that refuses not just development but drama -- that refuses the arrow of time itself?
4. Is this piece in the same structural-self-proof category (Cluster A) as the Crab Canon and the Fifth Symphony?
