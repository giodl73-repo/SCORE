# Arvo Part on "Spiegel im Spiegel"

## Opening

I will say very little about this piece. Not because there is nothing to say, but because the piece has said it.

The mirrors are facing each other. What they contain is everything that two mirrors facing each other contain: the image of the image of the image, without end, within a space that is not without end. That is not a paradox. That is the situation.

I will answer the panel's questions as precisely as I can.

## Structure: 22/25

The tintinnabuli method is the structure. Not a vehicle for the structure -- the method IS the structure, the way a fugue subject IS the structure of a fugue. The melodic voice moves through the scale; the tintinnabulus voice rings the triad; the relationship between them is fixed by the method's rules and does not change. Every note in the piece is generated by these rules without exception.

This is architecture in the same sense that a canon is architecture: the rules generate the piece; the piece is what the rules make of the materials. The mirror logic is not decorative; it is the formal principle. The violin's sustained pitch is always already present in the piano's arpeggiated triad. The piano's arpeggiation reflects the violin's position in the scale. The two voices are mirrors.

I dock three points from my own piece. The transition back from the upper register to the lower register is the movement in the piece that is least fully determined by the mirror logic. The violin descends through the scale as it ascended; this is correct formally. But the descent is not the same experience as the ascent -- the listener has already heard the ascent, and the descent covers the same pitches in the same relationships. The piece could be argued to have said what it needed to say at the apex of the violin's ascent. The descent adds duration without adding new information to the mirror logic. I chose to include the descent because the mirror image requires it -- a mirror reflects symmetrically. But I acknowledge the cost.

## Craft: 23/25

The tintinnabuli method is craft of the most rigorous kind: not the craft of making many choices correctly, but the craft of finding the rule that eliminates wrong choices. Once the method is established, what remains is the choice of materials within the method: which key, which tempo, which dynamic level, which register, which instrument.

In this piece, every material choice is correct. A major is the key because A major is the key that sounds like this piece needs to sound -- neither too bright (C major would be too simple, too plain) nor too dark. The tempo is slow enough that each of the violin's pitch changes is an event. The dynamic level is quiet enough that the listener is not distracted by volume and is given the space to hear the relationship between the two voices. The instrumentation (piano and violin) is correct because the piano's attack and decay create a different temporal relationship to the sustained violin tone than any other instrument would.

Two points off: the piece does not develop its craft within itself. This is not a flaw -- it is the intention. But Economy of craft means, in part, demonstrating the capacity for craft choices that were not required. In "Spiegel im Spiegel," the craft is entirely in the preliminary choices; once those are made, the piece executes them without further craft decisions being required. I accept this as the correct relationship between composer and method in the tintinnabuli system, but I acknowledge that an outside assessor might find the absence of in-piece craft decisions a limitation.

## Originality: 14/15

The tintinnabuli method is original. No one composed this way before 1976. The application of the method to the mirror image is original within the method. The specific choices of A major, piano-violin, slow arpeggiation, and the extreme length of the sustained violin tones are all specific to this piece and are not implied by the method alone.

One point off: the sustained drone as a compositional device is ancient -- present in Indian classical music, in medieval organum, in Appalachian music. The tintinnabuli method is not a drone, but its surface resemblance to sustained-tone music means that its originality requires context to be fully appreciated.

## Resonance: 14/15

The piece creates what it sets out to create. I know this because I am present in every note of it and I know what I was looking for: the sensation of suspended time, the inner space that opens when attention is given to one thing long enough that it reveals everything.

I have been told many times that the piece is used in films about death, grief, and transcendence. This is not surprising. The sensation of suspended time is the sensation associated with those states. The piece does not produce those states directly; it creates the sonic conditions in which those states can be inhabited. A listener who is open to inhabiting those conditions will inhabit them. A listener who is not will hear sustained notes.

One point off: the piece does not guarantee the silence after it ends. A listener who is immediately returned to the ordinary flow of time after the final note will have experienced less than the piece is capable of giving. This is not the piece's failure; it is a condition that cannot always be met.

## Economy: 20/20

Nothing wasted. This is not modesty about my own work -- it is the consequence of the method. Once the tintinnabuli rules are established, waste is structurally impossible in the same way that waste is structurally impossible in a palindromic canon: the method generates exactly the notes it generates, and every note generated is what the method requires.

The only Economy question is whether the descent (after the violin reaches the apex of its ascent) is wasted. I have addressed this above under Structure: the descent is required by the mirror logic (a mirror reflects symmetrically), and I accept the three-point Structure penalty rather than the Economy penalty. The descent is not waste; it is the mirror's other half.

Economy 20.

## Innovation Notes

**Innovation #9: Economy through subtraction.** The Economy dimension currently measures whether notes are wasted -- whether notes present are unnecessary. In "Spiegel im Spiegel," the Economy question is different: the piece uses almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is not a limitation but the method's central achievement. Economy through subtraction means that the most economical choice is to use less -- not to optimize among many notes, but to discover that fewer notes communicate more. The rubric's Economy anchors do not currently address this: they address the economy of a note-rich texture, not the economy of a note-poor one. The distinction matters.

**Innovation #10: Structural stasis as spiritual argument.** The tintinnabuli method argues that attention to one thing, sustained long enough, reveals everything. This is not the same as Miles Davis's structural stasis (the refusal of development in favor of modal exploration) and not the same as Bach's palindromic structure (the mathematical proof). Part's stasis is a spiritual argument: the sustained tone is not pointing elsewhere; it is fully present in itself, and the sustained attention to it is the spiritual practice the piece enacts. The piece IS the practice, not a description of it.
