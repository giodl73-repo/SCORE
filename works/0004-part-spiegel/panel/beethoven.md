# Ludwig van Beethoven on "Spiegel im Spiegel"

## Opening

I will be direct with this panel, as I am always direct, even when directness requires me to report difficulty I did not anticipate.

This piece is almost incomprehensible to me as music. I do not say this as a dismissal. I say it as a precise report of my response. I do not know what to do with ten minutes of A major arpeggiation over a violin that moves stepwise through a scale at one note per thirty seconds. I do not know what is happening in this music that could not happen in one minute rather than ten. I do not know why nothing develops.

And yet. I heard something. I must report it.

## Structure: 13/25

The tintinnabuli method is a formal system. I accept this. Rules that generate a piece completely are a form of structure. I accept this also.

What I cannot accept: a formal system that generates a piece without any structural differentiation. The piece begins at the beginning and ends at the end, and between beginning and end there is no structural event. No development. No recapitulation. No earned arrival. The violin moves from A to the top of the scale and back down. That is movement. But it is not structural argument.

My entire compositional life has been organized around the belief that form exists to create the conditions for drama -- that form is not the container for music but the music's capacity to surprise itself. This piece refuses surprise. It refuses drama. It refuses the arrow of time.

I am not able to score this as architecture in the sense I understand architecture. The mirror logic is elegant, but elegance is not architecture.

Thirteen points: the method is coherent and correctly deployed; the mirror logic is formally consistent. But there is no development, no earned arrival, no structural argument that requires what comes later to follow from what came before.

## Craft: 18/25

I am reviewing this against the tradition it claims: minimalism and tintinnabuli, not Viennese Classical. By the standards of its own tradition, the craft is high.

The piano writing is idiomatic in the extreme: the arpeggio pattern lies well under the hands, the 6/4 time signature is correctly chosen for the tempo and the arpeggio figure, the dynamic level is consistently achievable. The violin writing requires the player to sustain tones without vibrato, without slide, without the usual expressive techniques of the instrument. This is a genuine craft demand -- negative craft, as I would call it.

But I find the absence of counterpoint difficult to accommodate in a craft score. There are two voices. They are not in counterpoint. They move in parallel -- the violin's sustained tone floating above the piano's constant arpeggio -- without the kind of voice independence that I associate with craft of the highest order. The two voices are not in conversation; they are in parallel contemplation. This is, I understand, the intention. But I cannot award full craft marks for writing that systematically declines to develop the relationship between its voices.

Seven points off: five for the absence of developmental craft between the voices, two for the piano's unchanging arpeggio, which, while correct, is not an example of what I would call compositional decision-making within the piece.

## Originality: 12/15

The tintinnabuli method is original. I grant this without reservation. Part spent eight years in silence to arrive at it. What he brought back was genuinely new.

The specific application in "Spiegel" is original. I grant this too.

Three points off: not for the originality of the method, but for the narrowness of its deployment here. A method that is this rigidly constrained produces originality of a specific kind: the originality of having found the method. Within the method, the piece does not make many original choices -- it makes the most austere possible use of the method's resources. Austerity is a choice. But austerity as the only choice is not the same as rich and varied originality.

## Resonance: 12/15

I prepared myself to feel nothing. I felt something.

This surprises me. The piece has no drama, no development, no earned arrival. By every standard I hold for what music must do to produce resonance -- preparation, struggle, release -- it fails. And yet when the violin moved from one sustained tone to the next, very slowly, while the piano continued its arpeggiation, I felt something I do not have a name for.

Not drama. Not triumph. Something more like the experience of looking at a very still body of water and finding it difficult to look away. The stillness is the thing, not a failure to be moving.

I cannot give this piece the full Resonance score I would give the Fifth Symphony's first movement. The Fifth earns its resonance through combat; this piece produces its resonance through surrender. I am not sure surrender is the right mode for music. But I felt something, and I am reporting it.

Three points off: not for the resonance that arrived, but for the resonance that did not -- for the drama that was refused, for the arrival that was declined, for the listener I know who cannot stay still for ten minutes of A major arpeggiation and will not receive what this piece has to give.

## Economy: 15/20

This is where I am most challenged. The Economy dimension asks whether every note earns its place. In this piece, every note earns its place by the method's logic. The piano's arpeggiation is structurally required throughout. The violin's sustained tones are the method's melodic voice and earn their place by definition.

But: the piece is ten minutes long. Is it ten minutes of economy? Or is it two minutes of economy repeated five times?

I cannot fully resolve this. I dock five points for the length, because I cannot convince myself that the ten minutes are not five times two minutes. The mirror logic requires the descent after the ascent; this doubles the length without doubling the content. I accept Part's argument that the mirror requires symmetry. But symmetry at this tempo and this density creates a piece that is twice as long as its formal argument requires.

## Innovation Note

This piece forced me to report something I did not expect: resonance through surrender. Music that refuses drama can produce resonance of a different kind from drama-produced resonance -- not triumph, not the feeling of having survived a war, but something closer to stillness-as-arrival. I do not know how to score this with the current rubric. The Resonance dimension asks whether the intended effect is achieved; this piece achieves an effect that the rubric does not have a name for. This is a gap I am naming for the panel.
