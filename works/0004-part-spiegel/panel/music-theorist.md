# Music Theorist on "Spiegel im Spiegel"

## Formal Analysis

"Spiegel im Spiegel" operates within the tintinnabuli tradition, which is a specific compositional system with its own formal grammar. Assessment of this work applies that grammar, not the grammar of Classical sonata form or modal jazz.

## The Tintinnabuli Method: Technical Description

The tintinnabuli method deploys two voices in a strict relationship:

**Melodic voice (violin/cello):** moves stepwise through the diatonic scale. May move up or down; may pause on a pitch; must remain within the diatonic pitches of the key.

**Tintinnabulus voice (piano, in this work):** plays only the notes of the tonic triad. The tintinnabulus voice does not develop, modulate, or depart from the tonic triad in any circumstance. It "rings" throughout the piece like a bell, fixed at its position.

**Relationship rule:** The tintinnabulus voice is positioned relative to the melodic voice according to a rule that does not change within a work. Part designates this relationship as "1st position above," "1st position below," "alternating," etc. The relationship determines which note of the triad is closest to the melodic voice and how the two voices interact moment to moment.

In "Spiegel im Spiegel," the piano's arpeggiated triad creates an expanded tintinnabulus texture: both hands arpegiate the A major triad in contrary and parallel motion, creating a sustained sonic field rather than a single voice. This is the tintinnabuli method deployed at the instrument's full capacity.

## Harmonic Analysis

The piece is in A major throughout. No modulation. The harmonic content is the interaction between the piano's arpeggiated A major triad (A, C#, E) and the violin's position in the A major scale at any given moment.

When the violin plays A, it is playing the root of the triad -- maximum consonance, maximum stillness. When the violin plays D (the fourth scale degree), it is playing the only pitch in the A major scale that does not belong to the A major triad -- maximum tension relative to the tintinnabulus voice, within the diatonic context. When the violin plays B, C#, E, or G# (the second, third, fifth, and seventh scale degrees), it creates intermediate degrees of consonance and tension.

The piece's harmonic "motion" is the slow traversal of these consonance-tension relationships as the violin moves through the scale. This is not tonal motion in the functional sense (there is no dominant preparation, no leading tone resolution, no ii-V-I logic). It is modal motion within a single tonal center: the violin explores the scale's degrees and their relationships to the tonic triad.

This is, technically, a form of tonal structure. Not the structure of sonata form or bebop chord changes, but a structure in which the internal hierarchy of the scale's degrees relative to the tonic triad creates a form of tension and release. The violin's arrival on the root (A) after passing through the fourth (D) is a release; the movement from the root through the third and fifth is a brightening; the movement through the upper part of the scale (sixth, seventh, octave) is an opening. These are structural events, though they occur on a timescale of seconds rather than fractions of seconds.

## Craft Analysis

**Piano writing:** The arpeggio pattern is extremely well-chosen. The 6/4 time signature creates two three-beat subdivisions per measure, allowing the arpeggio to traverse the triad notes with natural rhythmic support. The pattern in both hands creates parallel contrary motion that fills the register from bass to treble. The choice is idiomatic: the pattern lies well under the hands and can be sustained indefinitely without technical strain.

**Violin/cello writing:** The sustained tones are demanding in the specific negative-craft sense Miles Davis identifies: the performer must resist the instrument's natural tendency to ornament, vibrate, and project. A sustained tone played without vibrato and without dynamic fluctuation for thirty seconds is a genuine technical achievement. The piece asks players to demonstrate mastery through restraint, not through velocity or range.

**The mirror relationship between voices:** The piano's arpeggio is the structural container; the violin's sustained pitch is the content within that container. The container does not change; what it contains changes very slowly. This is formally analogous to (though quite different from) a cantus firmus structure, in which a sustained pre-existing melody in one voice is surrounded by elaborations in others. Here the elaboration (piano arpeggio) is in constant motion and the cantus (violin) is in extreme stasis.

## Innovation Note

**Innovation #9: Economy through subtraction** requires a specific technical formulation. In tintinnabuli works, the "notes" that are present are each structurally required by the method. The Economy question is not "are these notes necessary?" (they are) but "is the pitch material sufficient?" The method achieves Economy through radical restriction of available pitch materials -- not through careful selection among many options, but through the prior decision to exclude almost all options. This is a different Economy logic from the Economy of a fugue (where many choices are available and Economy consists of selecting the necessary ones) or the Economy of an improvised solo (where choices must be made in real time). Tintinnabuli Economy is the Economy of constraint: the constraint produces the economy. The rubric should be able to credit this form of Economy appropriately.
