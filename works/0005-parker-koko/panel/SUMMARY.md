**Gate: PASS**

Aggregate score 89.4 is well above the advisory threshold (60) and above the expected range (85-91). ## 3. Key Agreements

**Resonance: complete consensus (14 from all three primary reviewers).** The most remarkable single result in the founding sprint. Coltrane (14), Miles (14), Beethoven (14): all three score Resonance identically, for different but compatible reasons. Coltrane: "testimony -- you are present at something that happened once." Miles: "the resonance of the real -- the recording says this happened." Beethoven: "the resonance of the impossible made actual." All three agree that "Ko-Ko" achieves a specific kind of resonance unique to improvised performance: the resonance of the unrepeatable real event.

**Craft high (23-24) when scored on improvised criteria.** Coltrane (24), Miles (23), Performer (24), Music Theorist (24): all four reviewers who applied improvised-craft criteria score 23-24. Beethoven (20) applied composed criteria by necessity (the rubric provides no other anchor) and scored 5 points lower. This is the clearest evidence that the rubric needs an improvisation mode in the Craft dimension: the same performance, scored by the same dimension, produces a 4-5 point spread depending on whether improvised or composed criteria are applied.

**Originality near-consensus (13-15).** Coltrane awards the full 15: "Ko-Ko" demonstrated that something was possible that was previously theoretical; the demonstration is the originality. Miles (14) and Beethoven (13) both acknowledge the demonstration's significance while noting that the vocabulary was collectively developed and the "Cherokee" changes were not composed by Parker.

## 4. Key Tensions

**Tension 1: Improvised Craft vs. composed Craft -- THE founding cluster.**

This is the defining tension of the founding sprint's final work, and the most important rubric gap identified across all five panels.

Coltrane (24): "Improvised Craft is real-time Craft -- the ability to make the right choice before the wrong choice has time to be wrong."

Miles (23): "Finding-in-the-act is not inferior to planning-before-the-act. It is different. The rubric needs to say this."

Performer: "The craft of improvised performance has three components: motor execution at tempo, harmonic navigation without deliberation, and melodic logic under pressure. The rubric's Craft anchors describe none of these."

Beethoven (20): "I have been forced to apply composed-Craft criteria to an improvised work. I know this is incorrect. The rubric does not give me the alternative."

Music Theorist (24): "Assessed on bebop-tradition improvised criteria: precision of real-time harmonic navigation, quality of chromatic choices at tempo, degree to which melodic logic is maintained under pressure."

The 4-point spread between Beethoven (20) and the rest (23-24) is not a disagreement about Parker's quality. It is evidence of the rubric gap. All reviewers agree that Parker's Craft is exceptional; they disagree about what exceptional improvised Craft is. The rubric needs to resolve this.

This generates Innovation #11 (Improvised Craft vs. composed Craft) and constitutes the seed of Cluster C (Improvised Music). This is the most important cluster candidate of the founding sprint.

**Tension 2: Real-time architecture -- planned vs. found.**

Beethoven (18): "Structure is the planning of events, not merely the result of events occurring in the right order. The arc was found, not planned. I score the result; I dock for the process."

Coltrane (22): "Architecture that emerges in performance is architecture. It was found because Parker was capable of finding it. The capacity to find is the architecture."

Miles (20): "I dock less than Beethoven. The arc is real. But the Cherokee changes provide the skeleton, and Parker did not compose the changes."

Performer (21): "The head is architecturally clear; Parker's solo has genuine real-time arc and motivic development; the process-structure distinction costs points but not many."

Music Theorist (22): "The motivic development in the solo is verifiable in transcription and is genuine architecture, regardless of whether it was planned."

The 4-point spread (Beethoven 18 to Coltrane/Music Theorist 22) reflects a genuine philosophical disagreement about what "structure" requires: pre-planning (Beethoven) or achieved result (Coltrane/Music Theorist). This generates Innovation #12 (Real-time architecture) and is the second element of Cluster C.

**Tension 3: Does "Ko-Ko" generate the most innovations of any founding sprint work?**

Yes. "Ko-Ko" generates Innovations #11 and #12, which together constitute Cluster C (Improvised Music) -- the most consequential cluster candidate for SCORE's future development. Every improvised work that enters the SCORE pipeline after this panel will be assessed using criteria that "Ko-Ko" forced into existence.

## 6. Cluster Analysis

**Cluster C SEEDED: Improvised Music**

"Ko-Ko" is the first work to fully expose the SCORE rubric's inability to assess improvised music on its own terms. Two innovations (#11, #12) point at the same gap from different dimensions (Craft and Structure). The cluster requires one more work to confirm, but the founding sprint has generated sufficient evidence to name it:

**Cluster C (proposed): Improvised Music** -- Works in which the primary creative act is real-time improvisation rather than pre-planned composition. Cluster C requires:
- An improvisation mode in the Craft dimension assessing real-time harmonic navigation, motor execution at tempo, and melodic logic under pressure
- An extension to the Structure anchors acknowledging real-time architecture (architecture found while being made) as a distinct and assessable formal category
- Clarification of the Economy dimension for works where stopping criteria may be partly structural (the form tells you when to stop) and partly musical (the argument has resolved)

Cluster C is the most consequential cluster of the founding sprint. Every jazz, folk, raga, or other improvisation-based work in the SCORE corpus will be affected by its ratification.

## 7. Design Recommendations

**Recommendation 1: Draft Cluster C amendment immediately.**
The improvisation Craft mode is the most urgent rubric gap in the founding sprint. Draft proposed language before Work 0006. The language should add to the Craft anchors:

*"For improvised works, Craft is assessed against the standard of the improvised tradition, not the composed tradition. The assessment criteria are: (a) real-time harmonic navigation -- does the improviser correctly navigate the harmonic terrain at performance tempo, including unexpected changes and errors by other performers? (b) motor execution -- is the physical execution of the improvised line precisely controlled at the required tempo? (c) melodic logic under pressure -- does the improvised line maintain musical coherence and arc across the duration of the improvisation, despite having been generated in real time? A score of 22-25 in improvised Craft means all three components are at the highest level. A score of 16-21 means one component is slightly deficient or one moment of autopilot disrupts the search."*

**Recommendation 2: Add Structure note for real-time architecture.**
*"In works where architecture emerges in performance rather than being planned before it, the Structure assessment addresses the quality of the architecture found, not the planning that precedes its execution. The capacity to find a complete arc (building, developing, resolving) in real time is a structural achievement assessed on the same standards as pre-planned architecture."*

**Recommendation 3: Add Economy note for format-supplied stopping criteria.**
*"In improvised works, Economy assessment notes whether the stopping criteria are musical (the argument has resolved) or structural-formal (the form ends here). Works whose stopping criteria are entirely format-supplied receive Economy scores that account for this; the musical argument may not be complete when the format requires the improvisation to end."*

**Score note.** 89.4 is the correct v1.0 score and the highest aggregate score for an improvised work in the founding sprint. Under the proposed Cluster C amendments, the Craft score would rise from 22.95 to approximately 24.5 (Beethoven's score rising to 23-24 when assessed on improvised criteria), bringing the aggregate to approximately 91-92. This is noted for the record and is the expected consequence of ratifying Cluster C.

**Resonance note.** The unanimous 14 from all three primary reviewers on Resonance is the most striking single finding of the founding sprint. A work that produces identical Resonance scores from Coltrane, Miles, AND Beethoven -- three composers with completely different relationships to improvised music -- has achieved something remarkable. "Ko-Ko" is the founding sprint's highest Resonance scorer alongside Beethoven's own Fifth Symphony. This should be noted in the TRACKER.
