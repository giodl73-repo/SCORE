# Music Theorist — "Erbarme Dich, mein Gott" (BWV 244)
*Weight: 15%*

---

**Step 0 — Structural Approach: DEVELOPMENTAL (da capo aria form)**
Binary A-B-A form with da capo return. Key: B minor. Obligato forces: alto, solo violin, continuo. Duration: ca. 6-7 minutes.

**Harmonic Analysis**

The harmonic language is organized primarily through descending-fifth sequences. The opening progression — B minor → E minor → A major → D major → G major → C# diminished → F# major → B minor — moves through nearly the entire cycle of fifths in the first large phrase. Each arrival in a new key is a new position of grief, slightly further from home, until the approach to the dominant (F# major) allows the return to the tonic.

This sequence is standard baroque harmonic practice (the sequence of fifths, or *Quintfallsequenz*, was a fundamental compositional device in the period). What is notable is Bach's use of it not as a transitional passage but as the primary organizing principle of the entire A section. The harmonic movement does not develop in the classical sense — it sequences. The sequencing produces accumulation without resolution.

In the B section, the relative major (D major) is reached through a brief modulation at the midpoint. The relative major does not function as consolation — the harmonic color shifts but the emotional register does not. The B section's harmonic argument is: even in the relative major, there is no relief.

**Voice Leading**

The voice leading between the alto and violin is impeccable by Baroque standards. The two voices maintain full independence throughout: when the alto descends, the violin frequently ascends; when the violin sequences downward, the alto holds a sustained pitch. Parallel motion is used only at moments of intentional convergence — and these moments are harmonically prepared and dramatically significant. The moments of parallel convergence are the moments of maximum grief: the voices find themselves in unison for an instant and then immediately diverge, as if the union is too costly to maintain.

No parallel fifths or octaves in the upper voices except at one moment in the B section that functions as an intensifier rather than a lapse — the preparation and context make clear this is a choice, not an oversight.

The continuo voice is correctly subordinate. It maintains harmonic clarity without drawing attention. Its rhythmic pattern (generally eighth-note movement) provides the forward motion that allows both upper voices to sustain without the music stalling.

**The Violin Obligato**

The technical demands on the solo violin include:
- Double stop passages requiring simultaneous melodic and harmonic function
- Large-interval leaps within sustained legato phrases
- Sustained intensity through the entire movement with no neutral passages

From a technical standpoint, every demand is functionally motivated. The double stops are required because the violin is performing two emotional registers simultaneously — the melodic cry and the harmonic ground beneath it. The large leaps produce interval-affect of the kind the Baroque treatises describe — the upward leap as yearning, the downward fall as grief. The sustained intensity is the musical embodiment of the Affekt doctrine: one sustained emotion without deviation.

**Formal Proportions**

A section: approximately 4 minutes. B section: approximately 1.5 minutes. Da capo: repeat of A section (abbreviated in performance practice). The B section is shorter than the A by a significant margin. This is a slight formal imbalance — the convention expects more equilibrium between the sections. However, the content justifies the asymmetry: Peter does not have a long alternative statement to make. He has a single intensification. The brevity of the B section is a content-driven formal choice.

**Scoring**

**S — Structure: 23/25**

The da capo form correctly contains the content. The harmonic language of the A section is internally consistent and produces the intended accumulation. Minor reduction for the B section's slightly compressed proportions creating a visible formal asymmetry.

**C — Craft: 24/25**

Voice leading is clean and purposeful throughout. The obligato writing is technically demanding but functionally justified at every point of difficulty. The continuo is correctly subordinate. Minor reduction for one passage in the B section where the violin becomes more conventionally accompanimental than the high standard set in the A section.

**O — Originality: 12/15**

The assignment of a full expressive argument to the obligato violin — not virtuosic display but emotional embodiment — is an original refinement of the Baroque aria form. The forms and harmonic language are received. Ceiling Originality is not available for works of this type (see Shankar Raga Yaman, 0015: interpretive/compositional distinction; here the forms are received rather than invented).

**R — Resonance: 14/15**

The grief affect is sustained throughout without deviation. The intended emotional effect — sustained lamentation, specifically the grief of one who has failed at a critical moment — is fully achieved. Minor reduction from ceiling: the B section does not fully escape the emotional register of the A section, which is the correct choice dramatically but produces a slightly narrower Resonance range than a work that moves more decisively through contrasting affects.

**E — Economy: 18/20**

The da capo is structurally necessary. Minor deductions for two B section passages where the harmonic sequence extends one iteration beyond its natural cadence point.

**Total (Music Theorist, 15%):** 91/100
