## Step 0 — Structural Approach

**DEVELOPMENTAL (rhapsody form — free-form developmental)**

Analytically, the rhapsody form can be described as a through-composed developmental structure with thematic return. It follows a trajectory: A (opening theme) → transitional material → B (love theme) → development/transition → A+B (culmination). The lack of strict exposition-development-recapitulation rules gives the form flexibility but also creates assessment challenges: "development" in rhapsody form means "movement toward," not "transformation of."

## Review

I will examine the harmonic integration of the jazz and classical elements, which is the primary theoretical question this work raises.

**Blue notes in a classical tonal framework:**

The blue note tradition in African American music produces the flattened third and seventh (in a major key context) through a combination of microtonal inflection and implied harmonic simultaneity. The blues guitarist bends the string to a pitch between the major third and the minor third; the blues singer slides through the same interval. The "blue" quality is in the indeterminacy — the note is neither exactly the major nor the minor.

In orchestral notation, this indeterminacy cannot be directly represented. Gershwin notates blue notes as flatted thirds and sevenths — the minor third and minor seventh in a major key context — which is the nearest representable approximation. The result is modal mixture: borrowing from the parallel minor (or from the blues scale, which has its own modal character) within a major key framework.

This is not harmonically incorrect. Modal mixture is a recognized technique in classical harmony. The analytical question is whether the modal mixture in the Rhapsody serves a structural purpose (as it does in, for example, Schubert's harmonic language, where the flat-sixth degree creates a specific emotional effect at key moments) or whether it is applied as surface coloring.

My assessment: the blue notes in the Rhapsody are primarily surface coloring. They appear throughout the work without creating the structural harmonic contrasts that would make them architecturally significant. The love theme, for instance, is in C major with blue-note inflections; the opening theme is in Bb with blue-note inflections. The blue notes are consistent (a sign of harmonic language mastery) but not structurally differentiated (a sign that they are not doing architectural work).

**Syncopation as technique vs. syncopation as structure:**

Jazz syncopation operates structurally when the off-beat accent is the primary carrier of harmonic and expressive information — when the off-beat is where the important note lives. In the Rhapsody, syncopation operates as rhythmic energy within a melodic structure organized on the strong beats. The strong beat is still harmonically primary; the syncopation creates rhythmic momentum around that primary beat. This is syncopation-as-technique rather than syncopation-as-structure.

**The transitional passages:**

The weakest analytical moments are the transitional passages between thematic areas. These passages use sequential material (a motive repeated at different pitch levels) that continues beyond its analytical function. Sequential development is a standard classical technique; the issue here is that the sequences do not connect the jazz and classical elements — they use classical sequential technique with jazz-flavored surface material, without the integration going deeper.

**Synthesis assessment:**

The harmonic languages coexist rather than integrate. The jazz elements (blue notes, syncopation, improvisatory piano figuration) inhabit the classical framework without fundamentally altering its harmonic logic. A classical analyst could describe the Rhapsody without invoking jazz theory; a jazz analyst could describe it primarily through classical theory. The two analytical vocabularies are not unified; they are applied sequentially to different aspects of the same work.

This is the theoretical basis for the synthesis asymmetry that other reviewers will name from their traditions.
