# Bach reviews the WTC Prelude No. 1 in C Major, BWV 846

**Step 0 — Structural approach: MATHEMATICAL/FORMAL — harmony without melody proves it constitutes musical architecture**
Every bar is one harmony, arpeggiated. No melody. The formal constraint (one-chord-per-bar pattern, consistent throughout) is chosen precisely to remove all variables except the harmonic sequence. The harmonic sequence then constitutes the complete musical argument. This is a Cluster A candidate: the Prelude proves that harmony alone can constitute musical expression by being the demonstration of that proof.

I will be as precise as I can about a piece I know from the inside.

The Prelude No. 1 opens the WTC with the simplest possible gesture: C major, arpeggiated, one bar. Everything else follows from this. The structural principle is established in the first bar and does not change. The texture does not change. The pattern does not change. What changes is the harmony, and the harmony changes in a sequence that is complete, internally consistent, and formally conclusive.

**Structure — Cluster A confirmation:**

The Prelude's formal structure is a proof. It proves that harmony without melody can constitute complete musical expression. The proof works as follows: here is a piece without melody; it constitutes complete musical expression; therefore, melody is not required for complete musical expression. The piece IS the proof. You cannot paraphrase it without losing what it demonstrates.

This is the same structural self-proof category as the Crab Canon (structural palindrome proves temporal reversibility) and the Beethoven Fifth (four-note motif proves it contains a symphony). Three instances, three mechanisms: palindromic proof, developmental proof, harmonic-completeness proof. Cluster A's scope is confirmed: structural self-proof achieves the top of the Structure band through whatever mechanism the proof is accomplished.

I score Structure 24, not 25. The reason: the piece does not quite exhaust the proof's implications. A proof, once stated, can be demonstrated at increasing levels of complexity. The Prelude proves that C major harmony without melody constitutes complete expression; it does not prove that more harmonically remote material — material with more accidentals, more chromatic tension — also constitutes complete expression without melody. The full WTC makes this extended argument over 48 pieces; the C major Prelude is only the opening statement. Within the C major Prelude specifically, one point is withheld for the brevity of the chromatic moment (bar 23), which is harmonically the most sophisticated passage in the piece and resolves before it has been fully inhabited.

**Craft:**

The voice leading in the arpeggio pattern is impeccable throughout. Every chord is correctly voiced: the bass note in the appropriate octave, the upper voices arranged to produce smooth voice-leading connections between adjacent chords. This is not a simple accomplishment: the broken-chord pattern means that the voice leading must be correct simultaneously in the horizontal (the individual voices moving through time) and the vertical (the harmonic structure of each chord). Both are achieved at every point.

The chromatic moment at bar 23 is the technical peak: a diminished seventh chord introduced on a weak beat and resolved correctly into the approaching dominant. The voice leading through this passage — particularly the chromatic movement in the inner voices — is exactly right. No parallel octaves, no uncountable parallels, no moment where the voice leading produces an unintended roughness. I score Craft 25. I can find no technical error.

**Originality:**

The broken-chord prelude as a form was not invented here. Keyboard preludes in broken-chord style existed before the WTC. What is original is the use of the broken-chord texture as a constraint that removes all variables except the harmonic sequence — the decision to sustain the pattern for 35 bars without melodic interruption, trusting that the harmony is sufficient. This is original within the tradition, and it constitutes the structural argument I have described. I score Originality 12: genuine original contribution, falling short of the highest anchor because the form itself is inherited.

**Resonance:**

The intended effect — the sensation that harmonic movement can carry musical expression without melodic supplementation, that the listener can follow the harmonic journey as they would follow a melody — is achieved. The listener who attends to the Prelude's harmonies finds that they follow them with the same interest they would bring to a melodic line. This is the felt proof of the structural claim.

I score 13, not the maximum. The Resonance-proof gap (Innovation #3, from the Crab Canon review) applies here as well: the formal proof and the felt experience are related but not identical. A listener who does not know that the Prelude has no melody will hear it as complete. A listener who knows this — who is listening for what is missing — may find that the knowledge of the absence creates a slight interference with the pure experience of the harmonic journey. The piece proves its claim; the listener who is attending to the proof may not be fully receiving the music while doing so.

**Economy:**

Perfect. Every bar earns its place. The 35-bar duration is exactly what the harmonic journey requires: not one bar more, not one bar fewer. The pedal point at bars 32-35 is exactly as long as needed to prepare the final tonic resolution. The decision to maintain the same arpeggio pattern throughout means that no bar can be wasted on textural variation — the harmony must justify itself, in every bar, as sufficient content. It does.

I award 20. The structural self-proof note from v1.1 applies: works that prove their formal claim by being the demonstration score at the top of the anchor band when fully achieved. The Economy is fully achieved. No note has been placed that is not doing structural work.

One observation for the panel: the Gounod addition (Ave Maria) does not damage the Prelude's argument. It confirms it. The harmony is complete enough to support any melody added to it — not because it is harmonically neutral, but because it is harmonically so rich that it can hold a melody it does not require. Gounod's melody is latent in the harmony; making it explicit is a different compositional act, not a correction of the Prelude's absence.
