# Pärt reviews the WTC Prelude No. 1 in C Major, BWV 846

**Step 0 — Structural approach: MATHEMATICAL/FORMAL — harmony without melody proves it constitutes musical architecture**
Every bar is one harmony. The constraint is total and produces its own logic. The piece proves something by being the proof. This is a mathematical/formal approach in the precise sense: the formal rule (one chord per bar, consistent arpeggio pattern) is applied without deviation, and the result constitutes the argument. I recognize this structure, though from a different angle than most. My own tintinnabuli method works by a similar logic: the constraint generates the music, and the music IS the constraint in action.

I approach this piece with something like kinship.

In my own work, I have spent decades pursuing the question of what happens when you reduce musical material to its minimum and sustain the reduction. When you use almost nothing, and what you use is the right thing, the almost-nothing fills the space completely. The Prelude No. 1 is not my music — it is tonal in the full Baroque sense, harmonically rich in a way my tintinnabuli works deliberately are not. But the principle underneath is the same: a constraint applied consistently, a trust that the constraint will reveal something rather than conceal it.

**Structure — Cluster A from the angle of constraint-as-proof:**

The Cluster A note in the rubric states: "Some works prove their structural claim by being the demonstration of that claim." The Crab Canon proves that musical meaning survives temporal inversion. The Fifth Symphony proves that four notes contain a symphony. The WTC Prelude proves that harmony without melody constitutes complete musical expression.

From my angle, the proof works through a specific mechanism I recognize from my own method: the constraint removes everything that is not essential, and what remains is essential. By removing melody — the most expected, most audible element of musical expression — the Prelude forces both the composer and the listener to attend to what is left. What is left is harmony. And harmony, attended to without the distraction of melody, turns out to be sufficient. The proof is the attending.

I score Structure 24. One point withheld because the Prelude is the opening statement of an argument the WTC will develop over 48 pieces. The proof is initiated here; it is not exhausted here. The full proof requires all 24 keys. The C major Prelude is the proof's beginning.

**Craft:**

The voice leading is without flaw. I will not enumerate the specific technical achievements because Bach has already done so; his review is more technically precise than mine would be. I will only add: the craft of this piece includes the craft of the constraint itself. The decision to maintain one arpeggio pattern for 35 bars, without textural variation, requires a specific discipline — not the discipline of resisting the temptation to vary the texture, but the discipline of having recognized that the texture does not require variation. This is craftsman's knowledge: knowing not what to add but what not to add.

Score 25. There is nothing to correct.

**Originality:**

The broken-chord prelude is inherited. The decision to deploy it as a proof of harmonic self-sufficiency is, to my knowledge, new in this form. The constraint that makes the proof possible — one chord per bar, no melodic supplementation — transforms an existing form into a new argument. Score 13. The form is old; the argument is new; the highest Originality score requires that the tradition have no words at all for what is being said, and the tradition had words for broken-chord preludes.

**Resonance:**

The piece achieves the effect I would call contemplative completeness: the listener who gives themselves to the harmonic journey finds that the journey is enough. There is nowhere else to go. This is the same quality that I pursue in my own work — the sense that attention to one thing, sustained and uninterrupted, reveals everything. The Prelude achieves this within 35 bars of tonal harmony, which is a different vehicle than my tintinnabuli method but the same destination.

Score 14. The point withheld: the piece works through accumulated harmonic attention rather than immediate emotional impact. A listener who approaches it expecting melodic interest will find it initially austere, and their resistance to the austerity may temporarily prevent the harmonic journey from working. The piece requires a specific quality of receptive attention that not every listener arrives with. This is not a failure of the piece — it is a property of the proof — but it is a slight limitation on the universality of the Resonance.

**Economy:**

The economy here is, as Bach's own review says, perfect. I will add only: the economy includes the economy of the constraint itself. The decision not to add melody, not to vary the texture, not to interrupt the pattern — these are economical decisions of the highest order. Economy, for my own work, is the prior decision to exclude, the discipline applied before composition begins. The Prelude demonstrates this prior-exclusion economy: the melody was excluded before bar 1 was written, and the exclusion is what generates the proof. 

Score 20. No note is out of place.
