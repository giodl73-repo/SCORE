# Listener reviews the WTC Prelude No. 1 in C Major, BWV 846

**Step 0 — Structural approach: MATHEMATICAL/FORMAL — harmony without melody proves it constitutes musical architecture**
I don't know what mathematical/formal means. What I know is that this piece sounds like water — not metaphorically, literally — and that I followed it all the way through without knowing where I was going or why I needed to keep listening. I kept listening. That's the evidence I can give.

**Resonance:**

There is no tune in this piece. I noticed this immediately. Usually when I listen to music I have a melody I can follow — I know where I am because I know where the tune is going. This piece has no tune. But I did not feel lost. I felt like I was following something else — not a melody, but the changes behind where a melody would be. Like following the light changing behind a curtain. You never see what's directly on the other side, but the light tells you what's happening.

I followed the light all the way through. When the piece arrived at its quiet end, I felt that something had been completed — not a tune, but a journey. The journey was harmonic. I don't have a word for what I followed, but I followed it, and it arrived somewhere, and that felt right.

Score 14. One point withheld because the opening section — the first several bars of C major establishment — was slightly longer than I needed to start following. I understood the piece's texture by bar 2. Bars 3 and 4 of the same thing were patient but slightly more patient than I needed. A small complaint about a piece I found genuinely beautiful.

**Economy:**

The piece is exactly as long as it needs to be. One point withheld for the same reason as the Resonance dock: the opening C major bars felt slightly more than the minimum necessary. Everything after bar 4 was necessary. Nothing after bar 4 could be removed without loss.

The consistency of the pattern — the same rippling figure throughout — that might sound monotonous and it isn't. It doesn't become monotonous because each bar is a different harmonic place. The pattern is the vehicle; the harmony is the destination. Once I understood this (by about bar 5), I was completely engaged. The pattern is the most economical possible vehicle: it focuses attention on the only thing that changes, which is the harmony, which is the music.
