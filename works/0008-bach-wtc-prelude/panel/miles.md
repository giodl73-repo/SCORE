# Miles reviews the WTC Prelude No. 1 in C Major, BWV 846

**Step 0 — Structural approach: MATHEMATICAL/FORMAL — harmony without melody proves it constitutes musical architecture**
One chord per bar. No melody. The harmony IS the music. I know what this piece is doing — what it proves — because I spent my career working in the same space, from the other direction: what can melody do without chords (the open space)? Bach is asking what harmony can do without melody. We were asking different versions of the same question.

The harmony is the melody.

That's it. That's the piece. That's what it proves. When I played Kind of Blue, I was asking: what can a melody do when it's free of the chord changes? When the changes don't tell the melodic line where to go, where does it want to go? Bach is asking the reverse: what can the chord changes do when there's no melodic line? When the melody doesn't tell the harmony what it's expressing, what does the harmony express on its own?

The answer, in both cases, is: everything. The melody, freed of the changes, is still a melody. The harmony, freed of the melody, is still music. We both proved it by making the proof.

**Structure — Cluster A from the angle of the other side:**

When the Crab Canon came up in the founding sprint, I said: "You were looking for the thing that doesn't change when time reverses." That's what the self-proof does — it finds the thing that still works when you remove something everyone thought was necessary. Bach removed melody from music and found that the music still works. This is the same move I made when I removed fast-moving chord changes from jazz improvisation and found that the improvisation still works. We both found the thing that wasn't necessary. Finding what isn't necessary is the proof that what remains is sufficient.

I score Structure 23. One point withheld: the proof of C major doesn't fully prove every key. The argument that C major harmony without melody constitutes complete expression is the opening argument of a system that requires all 24 keys to be complete. Within C major, the proof is made. I want to see it made in every key before I award the maximum.

Two points withheld for a second reason: the formal structure is slightly overlong at the pedal point. Three bars of dominant pedal is one bar more than the proof requires. The resolution of the proof could have arrived at bar 33 instead of bar 35. I hear those last two bars as slightly outside the structural necessity.

**Craft:**

The craft is very high. The voice leading is correct throughout — I don't hear it as technically as Bach hears it, but I hear it the way I hear it: nothing fights itself, everything supports the harmony, the harmonic changes arrive without preparation problems and leave without resolution problems. One point docked because the chromatic moment at bar 23 resolves more quickly than I would prefer. For me, the harmonic tension at bar 23 is the point where the piece is most alive — the furthest from home, the most in need of the resolution that's coming. I would have let it breathe two bars instead of one. The resolution arrives too efficiently at that moment.

**Originality:**

The broken-chord prelude is an old form. What's new: using it to make a proof. I've talked about this in every panel that has a Cluster A work — the self-proof is the originality, not the form. Score 12. The proof is real; the form is ancient.

**Resonance:**

The harmony is the melody. And the harmony produces in me something specific: attention to the changes, curiosity about where the next change goes, satisfaction when it arrives where it needs to arrive. This is harmonic following — following the harmonic journey the way you follow a melodic line — and it works. The piece produces the effect it sets out to produce. The listener who gives themselves to the harmonic journey finds that the journey is sufficient.

Score 14. The piece achieves its proof as felt experience. The listening that follows harmony instead of melody is a specific, real kind of musical listening, and the piece produces it. One point withheld: the opening bars (tonic establishment) are slightly longer than they need to be to produce the necessary stillness before the harmonic motion begins. Four bars of C major before movement starts; three would have been enough. The silence — the stillness — would have been more productive with one fewer bar of preparation.

**Economy:**

The one that's right is right. No note does less than its full job. The arpeggio pattern is the most economical possible texture for a piece that needs to remove melody from the equation: it sustains the harmony continuously without adding anything that competes with the harmonic content. The pedal point is slightly long, as I said — but within this, nothing is wasted.

Score 20, because the formal economy of the constraint itself (one pattern, applied consistently for 35 bars, generating all the musical content through the harmonic sequence alone) is the economy at the highest level. The economy of not-adding-melody is the economic act. Everything that follows from that act is economical by design.
