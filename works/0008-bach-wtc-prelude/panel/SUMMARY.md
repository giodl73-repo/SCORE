## 2. Gate Status

**Gate: PASS — with CLUSTER A CONFIRMATION**

Score 94.4 passes the gate with distinction. Slightly above the expected range (88-93), consistent with the Cluster A confirmation elevating the Structure score. The panel unanimously confirms the WTC Prelude No. 1 as the third work in Cluster A (Structural Self-Proof), expanding the cluster's scope to three distinct mechanisms: palindromic proof (0001, Crab Canon), developmental proof (0003, Beethoven Fifth), and harmonic-completeness proof (0008, WTC Prelude No. 1).

**Cluster A is now confirmed at three works.** The cluster covers structural self-proof through whatever formal mechanism the proof is achieved. The definition holds: works whose formal structure constitutes a logical proof of their own claim about music; the proof IS the work; the work cannot be paraphrased without losing what it demonstrates.

## 3. Key Agreements

**Cluster A confirmation — unanimous.** All three primary reviewers name the structural self-proof from their own angle:

Bach: "The Prelude proves by existing. Every bar is one chord, arpeggiated. No melody. The harmonic journey is complete. That is the proof: harmony without melody constitutes complete musical expression. The piece proves by being what it claims music can be."

Pärt: "The constraint removes everything that is not essential, and what remains is essential. By removing melody, the Prelude forces both composer and listener to attend to what is left. What is left is harmony. And harmony, attended to without the distraction of melody, turns out to be sufficient. The proof is the attending."

Miles: "The harmony is the melody. That's it. That's what it proves. Bach removed melody from music and found that the music still works. The proof of C major harmony without melody constituting complete expression is made. I want to see it made in every key before I award the maximum Structure score — but here, in C major, the proof is complete."

The three angles constitute the Cluster A convergence: formal completeness (Bach), constraint-as-revelation (Pärt), other-side-of-the-same-question (Miles). Cluster A's third confirmation is unanimous.

**Craft near-perfect (24–25).** Three reviewers award Craft 25 (Bach, Pärt, Music Theorist); Miles awards 24. The Music Theorist's detailed technical analysis identifies no voice-leading errors in 35 bars of through-maintained arpeggio texture. The near-unanimity of maximum Craft scores reflects the extraordinary technical achievement of maintaining flawless voice leading throughout a formal constraint that allows no textural adjustment to cover errors.

**Economy at or near maximum (19–20).** The formal economy is acknowledged by all reviewers as exceptional. The economy of the constraint (one chord per bar, no melodic supplementation) is the prior-exclusion economy that Pärt names from his own practice. The Listener is the only reviewer to dock any Economy point (one point for the slightly-long tonic establishment opening). All other reviewers award 20.

## 4. Key Tensions

**Tension 1: Bach vs. Pärt and Miles on Resonance.**

Bach scores Resonance 13; Pärt and Miles score 14. The gap is narrow but philosophically interesting.

Bach's position: the Resonance-proof gap (Innovation #3, from the Crab Canon review) applies here. "A listener who knows that the Prelude has no melody will hear it as complete. A listener who is attending to the absence — listening for what is missing — may find that the knowledge of the absence creates a slight interference with the pure experience of the harmonic journey." Bach docks one point for this potential interference.

Pärt's position: the piece achieves "contemplative completeness" — the sense that attention to one thing, sustained and uninterrupted, reveals everything. This is the full intended effect. Score 14.

Miles's position: "The harmony is the melody. The piece produces harmonic following — following the harmonic journey the way you follow a melodic line — and it works." Score 14.

The Listener confirms Pärt and Miles: "I followed the light all the way through. When the piece arrived at its quiet end, I felt that something had been completed." Score 14.

**Resolution:** The panel's Resonance consensus (13–14) reflects a genuine minor tension about the Resonance-proof gap rather than a substantial disagreement about the piece's effect. Pärt and Miles (and the Listener) experience the harmonic journey as complete without awareness of the absence of melody; Bach is attentive to the possibility that some listeners may be listening for what isn't there. This is the same tension identified at the Crab Canon. The panel does not resolve it but notes it.

**Tension 2: Originality calibration — how new is "harmony-as-melody"?**

Bach and Miles both score Originality 12; Pärt scores 13. The one-point gap reflects Pärt's argument that the use of constraint as proof — the prior-exclusion economy that generates the argument from its own structure — is specifically original in a way that distinguishes the Prelude from other broken-chord keyboard pieces. Bach and Miles agree the form is inherited but the proof-use is new; they disagree with Pärt about whether the proof-use alone warrants a 13 rather than a 12.

**Resolution:** Panel at 12–13. The broken-chord prelude form is old; the use of the form as a structural proof of harmonic self-sufficiency is new within the tradition; neither quality pushes into the highest Originality anchor (saying something the tradition had no words for at all). The 12–13 range is appropriate.

## 7. Design Recommendations

**Recommendation 1: Update Cluster A note to include third confirmed work.**
The v1.1 rubric Cluster A note reads: "Confirmed: Bach Crab Canon (0001) + Beethoven 5th (0003)." Update to: "Confirmed: Bach Crab Canon (0001) + Beethoven 5th (0003) + Bach WTC Prelude No. 1 (0008)." A proposed v1.2 amendment.

**Recommendation 2: Add harmonic-following as a recognized listening mode.**
The Resonance dimension currently describes the intended effect in terms of "joy produces joy, grief produces grief, transcendence produces something close to it." Harmonic-following — the mode of listening engagement produced by works whose effect depends on tracking harmonic motion rather than melodic content — is a distinct intended effect that the rubric does not currently name. A Resonance annotation noting "harmonic-following as a distinct mode of musical engagement" would help future panels assess works in this category.

**Recommendation 3: Extend Innovation #2 (Bidirectional Economy) to cover prior-exclusion economy generally.**
Innovation #2, from the Crab Canon review, noted economy that is "structurally guaranteed by the formal rule." Innovation #18, from this review, names the broader phenomenon: prior-exclusion economy — economy achieved by decisions made before composition begins. Both Innovations point at the same rubric gap: economy that is a property of the constraint rather than of per-note compositional discipline. A unified rubric annotation for constraint-generated economy may be more economical than separate annotations.
