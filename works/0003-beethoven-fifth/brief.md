# Brief: Symphony No. 5 in C minor, Op. 67, First Movement — Beethoven (1808)

## Work

**Title:** Symphony No. 5 in C minor, Op. 67, first movement (Allegro con brio)
**Composer:** Ludwig van Beethoven
**Premiered:** December 22, 1808, Theater an der Wien, Vienna
**Duration:** approximately 6:30–7:30 (first movement, depending on tempo and repeats)
**Tradition:** symphony (Viennese Classical, extending toward Romantic)

## Personnel

Symphony orchestra: strings (first and second violins, violas, cellos, basses), woodwinds (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons), brass (horns, trumpets), timpani.

## Structure (First Movement)

Sonata form with extended development and extended coda:

| Section | Key | Content |
|---------|-----|---------|
| Exposition | C minor / Eb major | First theme (fate motif); transition; second theme in relative major |
| Development | Modulating | Motif fragmented; taken apart note by note; chromatic modulations |
| Recapitulation | C minor | First theme returns; second theme in C major (not minor) |
| Coda | C minor | Extended — development continues; motif reasserted |

**The fate motif:** G–G–G–Eb (short–short–short–long). Four notes. Or, depending on how you count, five notes — Beethoven begins with two bars of rest before the first statement, and some interpretations hear the opening as a five-bar phrase (not the expected four). This ambiguity is not accidental.

**The five-bar opening:** Beethoven writes a phrase of irregular length — five bars, not four — before the music settles into its metric pattern. This asymmetry is structural: it prevents the listener from finding the metric ground immediately, ensuring that the motif arrives before the listener knows where to stand.

## Tradition Context

Beethoven's Fifth stands at the pivot point between Classical and Romantic symphonic form. The first movement is arguably the most studied single movement in Western music. Its motivic economy — deriving an entire movement from four notes — became the model for symphonic thinking in the nineteenth century. Brahms, Schumann, and every symphonist who followed worked in the shadow of this movement's demonstration.
