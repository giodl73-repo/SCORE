## 2. Gate Status

**Gate: PASS**

Aggregate score 93.0 is well above the advisory threshold (60) and above any reasonable binding threshold. The Canon cancrizans passes the SCORE gate with distinction for a canonical-import review. The score reflects both its exceptional formal achievement and the genuine panel tension over Resonance — which is scored 11–13 depending on the reviewer's position on whether formal proof constitutes felt experience.

---

## 3. Key Agreements

**Structure 23–24 (unanimous at the top band).** All five reviewers locate the architecture in the 22–25 anchor range. The one point withheld by all primary reviewers is for the same reason: the piece proves its claim in 30 measures and stops, without exhausting what it contains. This is not a failure — it is brevity as a formal choice — but it is a cost that no reviewer feels able to ignore.

**Craft 24–25 (extremely high consensus).** The counterpoint is flawless or near-flawless by every technical standard. The one-point variance (Bach and Beethoven award 25; Miles and the Music Theorist award 24) reflects a shared observation: some of the apparent craft excellence is structurally guaranteed by the palindrome rather than freely achieved by compositional judgment. The distinction is noted by multiple reviewers without consensus on how much it should cost.

**Economy in the top band.** Bach and Miles award 20 (perfect); Beethoven awards 19 (formally mandated economy question). The panel agrees that the double-duty character of every note is genuine and remarkable, even if its source is partly logical rather than disciplinary.

**The piece proves something.** All three primary reviewers name the self-proof innovation from different angles. Bach: "The piece proves by existing." Beethoven: "The mathematics are the argument." Miles: "You were looking for the thing that doesn't change when time reverses." The panel unanimously agrees that this piece is doing something the SCORE rubric does not yet have a name for.

## 4. Key Tensions

**Tension 1: Bach + Miles vs. Beethoven on Resonance.**

Bach scores Resonance 13; Miles scores 12; Beethoven scores 11. The gap is narrow in point terms but philosophically significant.

Bach's position: the piece achieves its intended effect — the sensation of standing outside time — for a receptive listener. The brevity prevents this from deepening (hence two points docked) but the effect is achieved.

Miles's position: the piece creates "the sensation of being suspended in a moment where past and future are the same" — a felt, specific, real sensation. Score 12 for the same brevity concern.

Beethoven's position: the piece produces admiration before it produces feeling. The formal proof is complete; the emotional arrival is compressed. A listener who knows what is happening may feel something close to awe at the logic; a listener who does not know may feel only that the counterpoint is excellent. Beethoven scores 11 — solidly in the "solid resonance with minor misfires" anchor range — but insists that formal completeness and emotional completeness are not the same thing. The palindrome un-rings the bell, and un-ringing the bell is philosophically interesting but not the same as the drama of the bell rung and unable to be unrung.

This tension is unresolved. It reflects a genuine philosophical disagreement about what music is for — whether the communication of a structural truth is the same as emotional communication, or whether they are related but distinct modes of experience.

**Tension 2: The Originality question — palindrome as technique vs. palindrome as proof.**

Bach 13; Beethoven 13; Miles 12; Historian 13. Scores in close agreement but reasoning varies.

Bach: the idea of the cancrizans is ancient; what is original is its use as structural self-proof — the demonstration that musical meaning is not destroyed by temporal inversion.

Beethoven: similar; the distinctive move is the transformation of a demonstration into an argument.

Miles: the palindrome is ancient; the proof use is new "in a specific way." One-point dock reflects his sense that the newness is real without reaching the highest anchor.

Historian: the synthesis of ancient form with specific chromatic theme in specific historical context is original as a combination even where elements are inherited.

The underlying tension: is Originality the innovation of a technique, the innovation of a use of a technique, or the innovation of a philosophical claim made through music? The panel is not fully agreed on which of these the piece achieves and whether the distinctions matter for scoring.

**Tension 3: Does Economy mean the same thing when it is structurally guaranteed?**

Bach, Miles, and Beethoven all note that the economy of the Crab Canon is not the economy of the Well-Tempered Clavier. In the WTC, Bach chose to be economical, bar by bar, note by note. In the Crab Canon, economy is logically impossible to avoid.

Bach: awards full Economy marks (20) and registers the distinction as an observation rather than a cost. The choice of the form is the economical act.

Beethoven: docks one point (19) to mark the question as genuine and not settled.

Miles: awards 20. If every note is doing two jobs, that IS economy, regardless of whether achieved through discipline or form.

This tension is not about the piece's achievement but about what Economy means in the rubric. Candidate for rubric annotation.

## 6. Cluster Analysis

**Work 0001 is the founding entry. No prior works to cluster against.**

The Structural self-proof innovation (Innovation #1) is designated as the candidate seed for Cluster A in the SCORE innovation framework. A cluster requires 2+ innovations pointing at the same rubric gap across 2+ works. The gap — that the rubric has no way to credit a work that proves a formal claim through its structure rather than merely demonstrating it — is significant and likely to recur in other formally radical works.

**Cluster A (candidate, unseeded):** *Structural Self-Proof* — works whose formal structure constitutes the argument the work is making, such that the work cannot be paraphrased without losing what it demonstrates. Requires one additional work to form the cluster. Future candidates: Bach's Musical Offering ricercars, Steve Reich's phasing pieces (which prove something about auditory perception through repetition), Baroque puzzle canons, certain twelve-tone works where the row itself enacts the compositional claim.

The bidirectional economy observation (Innovation #2) is a candidate seed for a separate cluster or a sub-annotation to the Economy dimension. Likely to recur in panels reviewing palindromic works, mensuration canons, twelve-tone compositions, or certain minimalist structures.

## 7. Design Recommendations

The Canon cancrizans is the first work to complete the SCORE pipeline. Four innovations identified; three rubric questions raised.

**Recommendation 1: Annotate the Economy dimension for structurally mandated vs. achieved economy.**
Suggested annotation: "In works where economy is structurally mandated, score the choice of the form as the economical act, and the degree to which the formal economy is realized without remainder as the scoring criterion." This preserves the full Economy range for formally mandated works while acknowledging the different nature of the achievement.

**Recommendation 2: Add a Resonance qualifier for formal-proof pieces.**
For pieces where the intended effect is the intellectual experience of a formal proof, the question should also be: does the listener feel the proof as necessity, or only understand it as information? These are different experiences. The rubric should be able to distinguish them.

**Recommendation 3: Name the structural self-proof innovation as a tracked category.**
If a second work in the SCORE corpus deploys structural self-proof, the cluster should be named and a rubric amendment considered. The amendment would add a modifier to the Originality dimension for works that constitute formal proofs through their structure.

**Recommendation 4: Consider audience calibration for Economy.**
The Historian's observation that the Crab Canon is economical for the most demanding possible single listener, not for a general public, raises a question about whether Economy should be assessed against the intended audience. The rubric currently treats Economy as an absolute property; it may need to acknowledge the audience-relative dimension.

**Founding tone note.** The Canon cancrizans sets the tone for SCORE panel reviews. The aggregate score of 93.0 is appropriate for a founding canonical-import: high enough to reflect the work's genuine achievement, not so high as to establish an impossible standard for future works. The Canon cancrizans is a ceiling piece for its formal category. Other works reviewed by this panel will be measured against their own traditions, not against the Crab Canon's formal perfection within its specific formal constraint.
