# Miles Davis reviews the Canon cancrizans

| Dimension | Score | Max |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| Structure | 23 | 25 |
| Craft | 24 | 25 |
| Originality | 12 | 15 |
| Resonance | 12 | 15 |
| Economy | 20 | 20 |
| **Total** | **91** | **100** |

---

Economy 20. Every note is doing two jobs at once. I don't give full marks often. This is full marks.

The palindrome is old. Medieval. You already know that. Bach didn't invent it. What Bach did — which is different — is use it as a proof. That's the move. Most people who write a palindrome are saying: look what I can do. Bach is saying: look what this means. There's a gap between those two things, and Bach crossed it. Originality 12. The idea is not new. What he did with the idea is new in a specific way. That specific way is worth twelve.

Resonance 12. Here's what the piece does to a listener who is paying attention: it creates the sensation of being suspended in a moment where past and future are the same. Not confused — suspended. You're inside the thing and the thing is going in both directions and you're not lost, you're still. That's a specific feeling. It's a real feeling. Twelve.

Structure 23. One point off because the piece is shorter than what it's claiming. You want to make a claim about the nature of musical time? You get thirty measures? I would have wanted more time inside the thing before it ended. Not because the palindrome fails — it doesn't fail — but because the sensation I described above has barely started when the piece is over.

Craft 24. The voice leading is right. The dissonances work. One point off because the craft constraints are so tight that it's hard to separate what Bach chose from what the palindrome forced. Good craft is partially about choosing. When you have no choice, some of what looks like craft is just the logic of the form.

Now.

You played the same thing twice but once it was backwards. What were you looking for?

You were looking for the thing that doesn't change when time reverses.

That's not a technique. That's a question. A real question, the kind that doesn't have an answer you can write down. You can only find out the answer by playing the music and seeing if it still works when you flip it. Bach flipped it. It worked. That's the answer. The answer IS the piece.

Good question.

The rest of what I could say I won't say. It's all in there. You just have to listen both ways.
