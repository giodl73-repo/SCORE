# Music Theorist reviews the Canon cancrizans

*Lens review: Structure and Craft scored. Other dimensions noted but not primary.*

The Canon cancrizans presents a unique analytical challenge: the counterpoint between its two voices is not freely composed but logically derived — Voice 2 is the exact retrograde of Voice 1. This means that every contrapuntal observation must be evaluated simultaneously in both temporal directions.

**Voice leading analysis.** The voice leading is correct by the standards of Baroque strict counterpoint in all passages I have examined closely. The two voices maintain their independence — they cross infrequently and when they do, the crossing is prepared and the resulting voice exchange is harmonically transparent. Parallel octaves and fifths are avoided throughout. The most striking feature of the voice leading is the behavior of dissonances at and near the midpoint.

In measures approaching the midpoint (approximately measures 12-18 in a 30-measure realization), the harmonic intervals between the two voices become most chromatic, because both voices are drawing from the chromatically dense interior of the royal theme. Here the dissonances are handled through a remarkable mechanism: each dissonance is simultaneously a suspension resolving forward (in the reading of Voice 1) and a suspension resolving backward (in the reading of Voice 2). The interval of a diminished seventh, which appears at the harmonic center, is treated with particular care: it is neither prepared as a standard suspension nor treated as a freely introduced dissonance, but is instead arrived at through the bidirectional logic of the palindrome, where both voices have been approaching it from their respective temporal directions for fifteen measures.

**The retrograde-specific harmonic implication.** There is one moment in the piece that the forward reading alone would not produce — and this is the most theoretically significant observation I can offer. Near the midpoint, Voice 2's retrograde motion creates a descending chromatic line in parallel with an ascending chromatic line in Voice 1. This contrary chromatic motion — which would require deliberate compositional effort to produce in free counterpoint — emerges here as a logical consequence of the palindromic structure. The contrary motion resolves the accumulated chromatic tension of the midpoint more cleanly than any freely composed resolution at this moment could have achieved. The form has produced a solution that the composer could not have designed more elegantly by hand.

This is a theoretically interesting property: the palindromic constraint generates harmonic solutions that free composition would find difficult to match. The constraint is not limiting; it is generative.

**Structural assessment.** Structure 24. The architecture is formally complete: the piece begins, reaches its palindromic center, and returns (via the retrograde of the opening) to its point of departure. The formal closure is total — there is nothing left unresolved by the final measure. The one point I withhold is for the brevity: the palindrome works in thirty measures, but it does not have time to demonstrate the full harmonic range of the royal theme's chromatic implications. A longer realization — perhaps with a middle section in the relative major before the retrograde of the opening — would have given the form more room to prove what it contains. As it stands, the formal argument is complete but compact to the point of compression.

**Craft assessment.** Craft 24. The contrapuntal writing is technically extraordinary. Writing correct counterpoint in one temporal direction is the basic requirement of the tradition; writing counterpoint that is correct in both temporal directions simultaneously is a different order of technical achievement. The one point I withhold reflects the inherent limitation of the palindromic constraint: some of the craft apparent in the voice leading is the craft of the form rather than of the composer's moment-to-moment decision-making. It is impossible, from analysis alone, to distinguish what Bach chose from what the palindrome necessitated. In a freely composed piece, every correct decision is a credited decision. In the Crab Canon, some correct decisions are foregone conclusions. The craft is real; the distinction matters.
