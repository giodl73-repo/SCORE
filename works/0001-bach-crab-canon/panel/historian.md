**Historical placement.** The Canon cancrizans belongs to the last decade of Bach's compositional life, a period characterized by formal extremity and self-referential systematism. The *Musical Offering* (1747), the *Art of Fugue* (BWV 1080, worked on until his death in 1750), and the *Goldberg Variations* (BWV 988, 1741) constitute a late-period trilogy of works in which Bach seems less interested in the immediate communicative function of music than in demonstrating — to himself, to posterity, to whatever organizing intelligence he believed was listening — what counterpoint can become when taken to its logical limits. The Canon cancrizans is the most formally radical piece in this trilogy: it is shorter than anything in the *Art of Fugue*, more purely abstract than any of the Goldberg variations, and more literally self-referential than either.

**The cancrizans tradition.** Retrograde canons have existed since at least the 14th century. Guillaume de Machaut's *Ma fin est mon commencement* (circa 1340-1360) is the most famous medieval cancrizans: the tenor voice is the retrograde of the treble, and the motetus is the retrograde of itself at the midpoint. Bach certainly knew this tradition, though whether he knew Machaut's specific work is uncertain. What is certain is that the cancrizans was a recognized form — a contrapuntal artifice — and that Bach's use of it in the *Musical Offering* was a conscious deployment of an ancient technique in a specific context.

The crucial historical distinction: Bach's use is not traditional. Medieval cancrizans were often composed as musical puzzles — mensuration canons and retrograde canons that required the performer to figure out the implied voice from a single notated line. The Canon cancrizans inherits this puzzle tradition (it is notated as a single line that two performers read from opposite ends), but it is not a puzzle in the same sense. The puzzle has an obvious solution; the single notated line is a visual demonstration of the palindromic principle rather than a challenge to the performer's ingenuity. The visual form is part of the argument.

**The Potsdam context.** The historical circumstance of the *Musical Offering* is essential for understanding the Canon cancrizans's economy. Bach's visit to Frederick the Great in May 1747 was arranged by his son Carl Philipp Emanuel, who served at Frederick's court. Frederick played Bach a long chromatic theme and asked him to improvise a six-voice fugue on it. Bach obliged — for three voices, declining the full six, which would have required a theme more amenable to the task. He improvised a three-voice fugue on the spot and promised a six-voice fugue on a theme of his own choosing, which he performed the same evening. He returned to Leipzig and composed the *Musical Offering* — ten canons, a ricercar, and a trio sonata, all on Frederick's theme — and sent it to the king.

This context matters for the Economy. The piece was composed for one listener: Frederick the Great, the most musically sophisticated monarch of his era. Frederick was not a general public. He was a trained musician who played the flute daily, who had studied composition, who could hear what was happening in the counterpoint. The Economy of the Canon cancrizans is not the economy of a public performance piece, designed to be accessible to a mixed audience. It is the economy of a private demonstration: every measure is aimed at a single listener who can be assumed to hear everything. No note is wasted on explanation. No passage is extended to help the listener catch up. The piece speaks at the speed of mastery to an interlocutor who is expected to keep pace. This is a different kind of economy from what the rubric usually encounters.

**Originality assessment.** Originality 13. The cancrizans form is ancient; the royal theme is Frederick's. What Bach contributed was the specific use of the palindrome as logical proof rather than as formal demonstration, and the insight that Frederick's chromatic theme was precisely suited for this treatment. The combination of existing elements — retrograde canon, chromatic theme, structural self-reference — produces something that had not been produced before. The Machaut cancrizans is a formal curiosity. The Bach is a philosophical claim in musical form. The difference is not in the technique but in what the technique is deployed to argue. For this, 13 is appropriate: not the full 15, because the elements are inherited; more than 12, because the synthesis is genuine and original.

**Structure assessment.** Structure 24. Consistent with the panel. The formal architecture is complete; the brevity is a cost; the tradition is fully understood and the form is deployed with complete command.
