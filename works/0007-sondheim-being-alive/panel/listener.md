# Listener reviews "Being Alive" (1970)

**Step 0 — Structural approach: DEVELOPMENTAL (modified AABA song form, musical-theater)**
I don't know what AABA means technically, but I can hear that the song begins in one emotional place and arrives at a completely different place, having gone through something real in between. The beginning feels contained; the climax feels necessary.

**Resonance:** I'll say what I experienced, because that's what I'm here for.

The song opened and I felt immediately that I was listening to a person thinking out loud. Not performing — thinking. The music was slow and the words were careful and I wasn't quite sure whether the person singing meant what they were saying or was considering whether to mean it. That uncertainty was interesting; it kept me listening.

Then something began to happen. I can't name the technical mechanism — something in the music changed, the temperature went up, the person singing began to sound less like someone considering and more like someone who had found out what they wanted while they were talking. By the climax, I wasn't watching a person think anymore; I was watching a person reach. And the reaching felt real.

The quiet at the end surprised me. After the loudest moment, silence and simplicity. The same words again. And they meant something different. I knew they were the same words; they didn't feel like the same words. This is the moment that stayed with me after: the same words meaning something different because of everything that had happened between. I don't know how that works technically. I know it worked.

Score 14. One point withheld because listening to this song in isolation — without watching Bobby for two acts before it — made me aware that some of the climax's weight was coming from context I didn't have. I could feel that the song was reaching for something, but I wasn't sure I fully knew what. In the theater, after two acts, I imagine I would know. As a piece of music encountered without its context, the climax is very moving but slightly unmoored.

**Economy:** The song is exactly as long as it needs to be, with one exception. There is a section in the middle where the music settled and paused and I thought: yes, this is the pause before the final push. But then the pause lasted longer than I expected, and I felt my attention start to drift — not away from the song, but away from the urgency of it. And then the urgency returned, and I was back. The drift was brief. But it was there. That's my two-point Economy dock.

Everything else earned its place. The quiet beginning earned the loud middle. The loud middle earned the quiet end. The ending earned what came before it by being quieter than I expected and truer for that.
