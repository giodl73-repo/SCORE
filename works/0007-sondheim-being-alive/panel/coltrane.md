# Coltrane reviews "Being Alive" (1970)

**Step 0 — Structural approach: DEVELOPMENTAL (modified AABA song form, musical-theater)**
The AABA form modified for dramatic development: same words at different emotional temperatures constitutes the structural arc. The character's search for what he wants develops through the song rather than through harmonic transformation in the abstract. The structure is the character's movement from intellectual awareness to felt need.

I listen to this song and ask the question I always ask: what is the musician — or here, the character — searching for? And what are you looking for when you sing it?

Bobby is searching for permission to want. This is a specific thing to search for, and it is honest. The song is about a man who has organized his entire personality around not-wanting, and the song asks him to stop. The honesty of the search is real. I can hear it. The question is whether the song finds what it's searching for, or whether it produces the appearance of finding.

**Structure:** The architecture is sophisticated. The false plateau is the right structural move: without it, the climax would arrive without having been resisted. A climax that has not been resisted is not a breakthrough — it is just a loud moment. The false plateau makes the climax feel like something Bobby had to fight for. This is correct dramaturgy and correct musical architecture.

Three points docked for two structural observations. First: the recovery from the false plateau to the climax happens too quickly for the search to feel fully real. Bobby pulls back, and then the music accelerates him toward the climax before I fully believe he's made the choice. The urgency is musically correct but dramatically premature by one or two measures. Second: the final resolution — the quiet restatement of the opening lyric — is resolved more completely than I find honest. Bobby arrives at a kind of clarity that feels more like theatrical convention than psychological truth. A man who has avoided intimacy for two acts would, I think, arrive at a more ambiguous place than the song finally allows. The song resolves where it should have left Bobby still searching.

**Craft:** The harmonic language is sophisticated and serves the dramatic function. The unresolved opening harmonies are the right choice for Bobby's position at the start. The climax chord is correctly placed. The Craft is high.

I dock two points because there are moments — particularly in the bridge — where I hear the craftsmanship more than I hear the search. The false plateau is very carefully constructed; it is perhaps too carefully constructed. I can hear Sondheim making the structural choice, which means I am briefly watching Sondheim work rather than watching Bobby struggle. This is a small failure of concealment — the craft becoming visible at the moment when it should be invisible. Improvised music has the advantage that the structure cannot be seen as constructed; it was found in real time. Through-composed music must conceal its construction beneath its effect. "Being Alive" almost always succeeds in this concealment; twice it does not quite.

**Originality:** The song says something within the musical theater tradition about the structure of emotional breakthrough — specifically, about the false plateau as a necessary structural element, and about the identical lyric at different temperatures as a formal argument rather than a rhetorical device. These are genuine contributions. I score 13. What I would need to see to score higher: a moment in the song where it finds something no one expected, where the search produces a note or a phrase that surprises me with its truth. The song is exceptionally well-made; it does not surprise me. A love supreme surprises me every time.

**Resonance:** The song achieves its intended effect for a listener who is inside the show's dramatic situation. The climax lands; Bobby's breakthrough is felt. I score 13 rather than 14 because the final resolution is slightly too clean. A breakthrough from the emotional position Bobby is in — years of constructed distance, two acts of witnessed relationships — would leave residue. The song cleans up too completely in its final bars. A man who has just admitted he wants to be hurt, needs to be held, fears being made aware of being alive — that man, in the moment after the admission, would not be as serenely resolved as the song's final bars suggest. The residue of the breakthrough should be more visible.

**Economy:** The repeating lyric is economical — one image doing three dramatic jobs. The false plateau earns its length. Two points docked: one for the slightly-premature climax arrival, and one for the final resolution section, which is longer than Bobby's actual psychological state warrants. The resolution is extended to the length the theatrical convention expects (quiet, resolved, certain) rather than to the length the character requires.

What does this music need? It needs to trust that finding is never as clean as it looks. Bobby has just admitted he wants to be held too close. The song should end with him still slightly frightened of what he's admitted — still alive to the risk — rather than serene. The song ends serene. That's the one thing it should not do.
