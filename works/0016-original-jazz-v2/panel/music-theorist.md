## Review

I will assess the revision analytically, note by note.

**Bar 19: Leap to F over Fbmaj7**

The F is the major seventh of Gb major (the key center the bridge has been operating in). In the context of Fbmaj7 (analytically: Emaj7, enharmonic), the F is the raised seventh of the chord — a major 7th above the root. This is a tension note: the major 7th above the chord root is unresolved until it moves by step downward.

The leap from the preceding Ab (in bar 18) to F is a major sixth upward. Analytically, this is an unusual leap in a melodic line — major sixths are large intervals that create registral displacement. The effect is audible prominence: the leap commands attention in a way that stepwise motion does not.

In version one, this bar had stepwise motion through the chromatic descent area. The stepwise motion was correct but undistinctive — any competent melodic line could have been there. The leap to F is a specific choice that identifies this moment as the peak.

**Bar 20: F sustained over Ebm7-Ab7**

Analytically, the sustained F over the changing harmony creates a pedal tone (or pedal figure) in the melody. The F functions simultaneously as:
- 9th of Ebm7 (an upper extension, mildly dissonant)
- 6th of Ab7 (a color tone, moderately stable)
- The lingering major 7th of Gb major (the tonal memory of the preceding chord function)

The simultaneous harmonic identities create analytical richness: the listener's ear cannot fully resolve which harmonic identity is operative, because the melody is not helping. The melody is staying; the harmony is moving. This is a well-established compositional technique — melody over bass ostinato is its most common form — and it is analytically correct here.

**Bar 21: F → Eb → Db (chromatic descent)**

The descent F → Eb → Db is a two-step chromatic descent. Analytically:
- F → Eb: major second downward (diatonic in Gb major)
- Eb → Db: major second downward (diatonic in Gb major)

The result is a two-step stepwise descent through Gb major's upper tetrachord. This is not chromatic in the strict sense — both steps are diatonic — but it creates a descending motion that parallels the A section's inner-voice descent (Eb-D-Db in the A section, which uses a chromatic passing tone: D natural, which is non-diatonic in Eb major).

The analytical connection is motivic rather than exact: the contour (stepwise descent through two notes, arriving at the b7 of the current key center) is parallel. In the A section: the inner voice descends through Eb-D-Db (the chromatic D is the color tone); in the bridge resolution: the melody descends through F-Eb-Db (all diatonic in Gb major, but the arrival on Db is the root of the dominant seventh chord Db7, which is the b7 of Eb major).

This motivic parallel is analytically intentional and analytically correct. It connects the bridge resolution to the A section's distinctive idea through contour similarity rather than exact pitch repetition, which is the appropriate relationship between a main theme and its development.

**The Fbmaj7 flag (from version 1):**

In work 0011, I flagged the Fbmaj7 chord (bar 19 of the bridge) as analytically unusual — not diatonic to Gb major or its parallel minor. I noted that it creates a chromatic mediant relationship within the bridge. In version two, this chord remains. I take it as intentional: consistent with the piece's flat-side chromaticism, and now better motivated because the melodic peak (the leap to F) provides the melodic justification for the harmonically unusual chord. The F in the melody is the natural (major) seventh of Fbmaj7, which creates a conventional major 7th sonority — harmonically stable within the chord's own terms. The chord was analytically unusual in version one; in version two, the melody justifies it.
