## Review

Version one's primary practical concern: the bridge peak in bars 23-24 was melodically underwritten. The performer was given a harmonically tense moment (Bb7(#5)) and a melodically plain option (plain Eb). A great performer would make something of it anyway; an intermediate performer would play the plain Eb and the moment would pass without landing.

Version two addresses this directly.

**The leap in bar 19:**

A major sixth upward to F. Practical note: the leap requires preparation — the performer needs to hear the F in the bar before the leap, because arriving on a major 6th upward without preparation is technically risky (especially for brass players). The chord on the preceding beat gives the F as a available color tone; a good performer will have it in their ear before the leap happens. The leap is achievable for any player at the advanced level; at the intermediate level, it requires careful attention in rehearsal.

**The sustained F:**

Two beats of sustained F over Ebm7-Ab7. This is the most valuable change from a performance standpoint. A sustained note at the peak of a phrase gives the performer:
1. Time to shape the note — crescendo, vibrato, tonal color choices
2. Time to breathe (if a wind player — though in a two-beat sustain with no breath typically needed, this is more about phrase shaping)
3. Emotional presence — the sustained note communicates sustained emotion; a passing note communicates motion

A great performer will swell the sustained F slightly, creating a subtle crescendo that emphasizes the tension before the resolution. This is idiomatic and natural; the harmony moving beneath the sustained melody provides the harmonic context for the swell.

**The chromatic descent (bar 21):**

F → Eb → Db. Three notes in the space of two beats. Achievable at the moderate tempo (quarter note = 124). The chromatic half-step descent is melodically natural — the ear follows chromatic steps easily, more easily than diatonic motion in many registers. The descent feels inevitable once the F is released.

**Innovation #24 revisited (Economy Divergence: Score vs. Performance):**

The original version created a scoring concern: the six-chord opening (bars 1-2) was compositionally economical but created improvisation density. The revision does not change bars 1-2, so this concern remains. However, the revised bridge peak creates a different relationship between score economy and performance economy: the sustained F gives the improviser a clear model for how to handle this moment in the solo choruses. A soloist who has internalized the composed melody will know that bar 20 is a "hold the tension note" bar — this is now in the composed head, where in version one it was not.

**Practical notation note:**

The leap in bar 19 should be marked with a crescendo hairpin beginning at the top of the leap and leading into the sustained F. The sustained F should be marked with a tenuto marking (indicating held duration, not shortened). The chromatic descent in bar 21 should be slurred. These markings are standard and communicative; the revision should include them explicitly.

**Overall assessment:**

The revision is an improvement from every practical standpoint. The leap is achievable; the sustain is a performer gift; the descent is natural. The chart is now more complete as a performance guide: the bridge peak was the one moment where the performer had to supply something the composer hadn't written; it is now fully composed.
