## Review

I said what I needed to say about bars 23-24 in the first session. The bridge peak was there. It was harmonically right. The melody let it down.

Let me look at what changed.

Bar 19. The melody leaps. Upward — a major sixth — to the F. That F is the major seventh of the Gb major area. The leap says: here. This is where we've been going. The stepwise approach in version one arrived without announcing itself. The leap announces.

Is the announcement the right move? I'll tell you when I've heard the rest.

Bar 20. The F is held. Two beats over Ebm7-Ab7. The harmony moves; the melody doesn't. The F is now three things simultaneously: the 9th of Ebm7, the 6th of Ab7, and the lingering major 7th of where we came from (Gb major). All three relationships are in tension. The hold gives the listener time to feel the tension rather than simply note it. This is the right choice.

Bar 21. The F releases downward: Eb-Db. Chromatic descent. Two notes. This is the inner-voice descent from the A section — the same chromatic motion that defines the opening of "The Long Way Back" — now in the melody, in the bridge, in the bridge's mediant key. The piece is saying: the same idea that you heard in the harmony in bars 1-2, you are now hearing in the melody, in Gb. The idea travels. It works here.

Does it fix the problem? Yes. The bridge peak is no longer melodically anonymous. The leap and the sustain give the moment the character it needs. And the chromatic descent that follows — the echo of the A section's distinctive idea — makes the bridge feel like it belongs to the same piece it departed from, which it did not fully do in version one.

Comparison to version one:
- Structure: the bridge now fully satisfies the bridge-as-necessity test. The final A returns with more weight because the bridge has done more. The bridge not only migrated the idea from harmony to melody; it demonstrated the idea in the mediant key. The final A is therefore the third demonstration: the idea in Eb major at the harmonic level (A sections 1 and 2), in Gb major at the melodic level (bridge), and in Eb major with the full weight of both previous demonstrations (final A).
- Craft: the leap is precisely chosen. The sustain is precisely placed. The descent echoes the distinctive idea precisely.
- Economy: the sustained F is not excess — it serves the tension. The descent is not excess — it completes the bridge-as-necessity argument.
- Resonance: the emotional arc is now complete. The bittersweet longing that the A sections establish reaches its highest point in the bridge's sustained tension, and the final A arrives with full resolution weight.

What I said in session 0011: the bridge peak was "melodically under-characterized." It is now characterized. The characterization is specific, not generic: a leap to the tension note, a hold, and a descent that echoes the piece's own distinctive idea. That is three decisions where the original made zero. Three is the right number for this moment.
