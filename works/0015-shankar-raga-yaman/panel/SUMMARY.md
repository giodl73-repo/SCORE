## Panel Consensus and Divergences

Highest consensus: Economy (18-19/20 from all reviewers) -- the alap's economy is unanimously recognized as exceptional. Part and Miles both award 19; both explicitly describe the alap as the most economical music they have encountered.

Craft consensus: All reviewers apply Non-Western Craft criteria (Innovation #31) and score 22-23/25. The tradition-specific assessment is consistent across reviewers from different traditions.

Originality: Unanimous 11-12/15. The raga is the tradition's, not Shankar's; interpretive originality within a transmitted tradition occupies a distinct tier below compositional originality. This is the lowest Originality score in the corpus for a canonical master's work -- but it reflects an accurate distinction between interpretive and compositional originality.

Part highest (91): Part finds the deepest kinship with the alap's meditative patience and the drone's structural role. His Economy and Resonance scores are highest, reflecting the closest philosophical alignment between tintinnabuli and raga practice.

## The Rubric's Non-Western Applicability

This is the first non-Western work in the SCORE corpus. Three findings:

1. The Craft dimension requires explicit tradition-specific criteria. The Western vocabulary (harmony, voice leading) does not apply; the raga tradition's criteria (raga grammar fidelity, meend execution, alap architecture, layakari) must be stated explicitly and assessed within the tradition's standards.

2. The Structure dimension requires a new sub-type: Modal-Revelatory (the alap). This is not captured by any current Step 0 category.

3. The Originality dimension requires a distinction between compositional originality (the work is the composer's own) and interpretive originality (the work is the tradition's; the interpretation is the performer's). Interpretive originality at the highest level occupies approximately the 11-12 range; compositional originality in an original work begins at 12.

## Innovations from This Panel

#31 -- NON-WESTERN CRAFT VOCABULARY

0015-shankar-raga-yaman : Part (primary: "the rubric's Craft vocabulary does not apply here; the Craft of the alap is grammatical fidelity + expressive depth + patience to reveal rather than demonstrate"); Coltrane (confirming: "the raga tradition's Craft demands were no less rigorous than my own -- only different"); Music Theorist (formal specification: "the tradition-specific Craft criteria must be explicitly stated and assessed against the tradition's own standards, not Western harmonic criteria"); Miles (confirming from Economy angle: "the discipline behind the economy is Craft in any tradition"); Historian (historical context: "Western attempts to assess non-Western music have consistently distorted it by applying the wrong standards") -- NON-WESTERN CRAFT VOCABULARY -- Craft -- The technical mastery in non-Western musical traditions is real and demanding but uses different technical concepts than Western harmony and voice leading; the Craft dimension's current anchors describe Western technical mastery and cannot adequately assess the raga tradition's gayaki, meend, gamak, layakari, and raga grammar fidelity; proposed rubric note: when scoring Craft for non-Western music, the reviewer must (a) identify the tradition's Craft criteria, (b) explicitly state them, and (c) assess the work against those criteria rather than against Western standards; note that Cluster C (Improvised Craft mode) established the precedent for tradition-specific Craft criteria; non-Western Craft vocabulary extends this principle to traditions outside Western music entirely.

#32 -- THE ALAP AS STRUCTURE

0015-shankar-raga-yaman : Part (primary: "the alap is architecture: the performer moves from lower to upper register, revealing the raga's character at each level; this constitutes form even without meter"); Coltrane (confirming: "the alap is the structural foundation, not the introduction; everything in the gat is a consequence of what was established in the alap"); Music Theorist (analytical specification: "modal-revelatory structure -- the progressive disclosure of the raga's full identity through note-by-note and phrase-by-phrase exploration -- requires a Step 0 sub-type of its own"); Miles (confirming from real-time architecture angle: "the alap is architecture discovered in the moment of building it") -- THE ALAP AS STRUCTURE -- Structure -- Unmetered, fully improvised exploration of a modal space, organized by the logic of which aspects of the modal space to develop and in what order, constitutes a form of structure that is simultaneously modal (fixed tonal center), improvisatory (found in performance), and developmental (progressive revelation); this is not captured by any current Step 0 sub-type: it is not Modal/Static (it moves and develops), not Improvised/Found (it follows a strict grammar, not free improvisation), not Developmental (it has no harmonic tension-and-resolution); proposed Step 0 addition: Modal-Revelatory -- structure built through the progressive disclosure of a modal identity, organized by the grammar of which notes and phrases reveal the modal character and in what sequence; canonical instance: the alap of Raga Yaman as performed by Ravi Shankar.
