## Step 0 — Structural Approach

**MODAL/STATIC with DEVELOPMENTAL sections**

The alap is Modal/Static. The gat is developmental within a fixed tala framework. Analytically, the two sections require different assessment criteria, which is itself a structural insight: a single performance has two structural sub-types operating in sequence.

The alap's structural principle: modal revelation — the progressive disclosure of the raga's full identity through note-by-note and phrase-by-phrase exploration. This is not the same as Western tonal development (no modulation, no harmonic tension-and-resolution) and not the same as Western modal stasis (it moves; it discloses; it develops its modal content even while maintaining its tonal center). It requires a structural sub-type of its own.

## Review

I want to address the rubric's applicability to non-Western music directly, because this is the primary question this work raises for the SCORE project.

**The Craft dimension's vocabulary:**

The v1.2 Craft dimension uses the following language: "voice leading is smooth and purposeful, harmonic language is consistent and expressive, rhythm and meter serve the music, texture is appropriate to the tradition and the moment, orchestration (if applicable) makes the most of its forces; every technical decision serves the musical result."

Of these criteria:
- "Voice leading" — not applicable in its Western sense (movement of individual voices in a harmonic context); applicable in a modified sense if interpreted as "melodic movement follows the tradition's grammar"
- "Harmonic language" — not applicable in the Western sense (functional harmony); applicable if interpreted as "modal grammar is consistent with the raga's established character"
- "Rhythm and meter" — applicable in the metered sections (tala framework); requires a different assessment frame for the unmetered alap
- "Texture appropriate to the tradition" — fully applicable: sitar, tabla, and tanpura drone are the tradition-standard forces
- "Every technical decision serves the musical result" — fully applicable: the tradition-specific criterion that every ornament, every note approach, every rhythmic choice must serve the raga's expression

**Tradition-specific Craft criteria for raga performance:**

The actual Craft criteria for raga performance are:

1. **Raga grammar fidelity:** Are the characteristic phrases correctly stated? Is the raised fourth (Ma#) properly approached in the ascent? Are the characteristic movements (Ga-Ma#-Pa, Ni-Dha-Pa) correctly executed?
2. **Ornament precision:** Is the meend (glissando) executed with the correct shape and speed? Is the gamak (ornamental repetition) rhythmically precise?
3. **Alap architecture:** Does the alap move from low to high register in the correct sequence? Are the characteristic notes revealed in the correct order?
4. **Layakari (rhythmic play):** In the gat section, does the improviser maintain relationship to the tala cycle while varying rhythmic subdivision? Does the improviser return to the gat at the correct rhythmic position?
5. **Note avoidance:** Are the notes that should be avoided in this raga (Ma natural, the natural fourth) correctly avoided throughout?

Applying these criteria to a canonical Shankar performance: the Craft is at the ceiling of the tradition. Shankar's meend execution is the standard against which other performers are measured. His alap architecture has been studied and described by musicologists as exemplary. His layakari in the gat is sophisticated without being virtuosic for its own sake — the rhythmic complexity serves the expression.

**The rubric gap:**

The Craft dimension's current language cannot assess this without substantial interpretation. I formally note this gap as Innovation #31: Non-Western Craft vocabulary must be added as a dimension note (analogous to the Improvised Craft mode added for Cluster C). When scoring Craft for non-Western music, the reviewer must first identify the tradition's Craft criteria, explicitly state them, and assess the work against those criteria rather than against Western harmonic/voice-leading standards.

**The alap as Structure:**

The alap is structurally unmetered, fully improvised, and organized by a logic that is simultaneously modal (fixed tonal center), improvisatory (found in performance), and developmental (progressive revelation of the raga's identity). No current Step 0 sub-type fully captures this. Innovation #32 (The Alap as Structure) names this as a new sub-type: modal-revelatory structure, organized by the logic of which aspects of a modal space to develop and in what order, without meter.
