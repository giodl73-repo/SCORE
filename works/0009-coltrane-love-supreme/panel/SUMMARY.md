## 3. Key Agreements

**Improvised-Craft criteria: unanimous application.** All four reviewers who scored Craft (Coltrane, Miles, Mingus, Music Theorist) applied improvised-Craft criteria explicitly, and three of them named the distinction from the rubric's composed-Craft frame independently. This is the most significant agreement in the panel and carries implications beyond this work. See Section 6 (Cluster Analysis).

**Economy docking: unanimous on the same passage.** Coltrane (18), Miles (17), and Mingus (18) all dock Economy for the closing collective improvisation after the spoken words. They agree that the structural argument concludes when Coltrane speaks "A Love Supreme" and that the music continues past that conclusion. This is exceptional agreement: three reviewers docking the same passage for the same structural reason.

**Twelve-key transposition: structural agreement across all reviewers.** All five reviewers accept the twelve-key transposition as a valid structural choice rather than an extended repetition. The agreement is not that it is developmental in the European sense but that its purpose — structural universality argument — is legitimate and correctly executed. No reviewer docks for the transposition sequence's length or for its non-developmental character.

**Originality ceiling: the Ellington constraint.** Coltrane (14), Miles (13), Mingus (14), and Historian (14) all dock one or two Originality points for the Ellington precedent in devotional jazz composition. The deduction is consistent and reflects genuine historical knowledge: Ellington was making sacred music in jazz before *A Love Supreme*; Coltrane's formal integration is deeper and more structurally complete, but the concept was not originated by him.

## 4. Key Tensions

**Tension 1: The Resonance question — whose intended effect?**

This is Innovation #19. The central tension of the panel is not between reviewers but between the Resonance dimension's current framing and the nature of this work.

Coltrane (15): "The intended effect of *A Love Supreme* is not a listener effect. The music is an offering to God. The primary audience is not the concert listener. If the rubric scores them separately — the offering and the listener's reception of the offering — this music scores at the ceiling on the offering and somewhere lower on the listener-reception question. But the offering is what the music is."

Miles (13): "The music creates the specific experience of being present at something directed elsewhere. This is the exact effect it intends. The devotional purpose is, for me, not a necessary component of what makes it extraordinary. I dock for the passages where the spiritual urgency narrows the music rather than widening it."

Mingus (14): "I know what it sounds like when a musician is doing something that costs them something — when they are not performing but giving. This music sounds like that. The giving is audible. The gratitude is audible."

The tension is real and productive: Coltrane scores 15 from certainty about the offering; Miles scores 13 from a position of aesthetic engagement without religious orientation; Mingus scores 14 from a position of moral seriousness without specifically religious framing. All three are scoring the same music, using the same dimension, with the same understanding of what the dimension asks — and they reach different conclusions because the dimension's question ("does it create the intended effect in the listener") does not fully fit the music's purpose.

This is Innovation #19 confirmed: the Resonance dimension needs a purpose classification that distinguishes devotional offering from audience communication.

**Tension 2: Structure classification — what IS the twelve-key transposition?**

All reviewers accept the twelve-key transposition. None of them can fully name it within the existing Step 0 categories.

Music Theorist: "I want to name this structural sub-type formally: universality through exhaustion. It is not developmental because there is no tension-and-resolution arc. It is not modal because no single center is sustained. It is not mathematical-formal because the formal relationship does not produce harmonic meaning the way a palindrome does — it produces a semantic argument about the nature of the material."

Coltrane: "The argument is not 'this motif develops into something else.' The argument is 'this motif is the same in all twelve keys simultaneously.' To make that argument through development would be to miss it entirely. The repetition and relocation IS the structure."

Miles: "The argument: this motif belongs to all keys. To make the argument, you visit all twelve. There is no shortcut."

Mingus: "This is the most original structural gesture I have encountered in this panel series."

This is Innovation #20 confirmed: the twelve-key transposition as structural universality claim requires a new Step 0 sub-type.

**Tension 3: Coltrane reviewing his own work.**

Coltrane scores 94/100 — the second-highest total individual score in the SCORE corpus to date (behind Music Theorist's 46/50 contribution to Ko-Ko, on a per-dimension basis). Is this generous self-assessment?

The internal evidence suggests not. Coltrane docks himself on Craft (familiar patterns, three moments of search-stopping), on Originality (the Shankar/raga debt), on Economy (the extended closing section), and on Structure (the abrupt transition). The one dimension where he scores at the ceiling — Resonance — is the dimension where he explains explicitly why certainty rather than generosity is the operative principle: he knows what the offering was and he is certain it is genuine. This is the same principle as Bach's Crab Canon scoring: the work either proves its claim or it doesn't; the proof is verifiable; the score follows from the verification.

Coltrane's 94 is a high score. It is not obviously inflated. The panel aggregate (90.4) reflects the other reviewers' somewhat lower assessments; the final weighted score sits where it should.

## 5. CLUSTER C CONFIRMATION

**CLUSTER C: IMPROVISED MUSIC — CONFIRMED.**

Cluster C was seeded at Work 0005 (Parker Ko-Ko, 2026-04-23). One additional improvised work was required for confirmation. Work 0009 (*A Love Supreme, Part I*) provides that confirmation.

**Evidence of independent naming:**

- **Coltrane** (30% weight, composer reviewing his own improvised passages): "Improvised Craft requires different criteria than composed Craft. The criteria are: correct for that moment, in that harmonic context, at that speed, without time to reconsider. Real-time Craft. I said this at the Ko-Ko panel, about Parker's music. I am saying it now, about my own. This is the second time this distinction has been named in a panel context."

- **Miles** (25% weight): "The criteria for improvised Craft are: can you navigate the harmonic terrain at tempo without hesitation? Can you maintain physical control of the instrument throughout? Does your improvised line have shape and logic that you found in the moment rather than planned in advance? These are different from the composed-Craft questions. They are not inferior questions. They are different questions. The rubric needs to say this permanently."

- **Mingus** (20% weight): "I am naming it here, independently, because I think the panel needs to confirm this from multiple angles: Coltrane's improvised passages in Part I cannot be assessed against the same criteria as a composed string quartet. The questions are different."

- **Music Theorist** (15% weight): "This is the second work in the SCORE corpus to require explicit application of improvised-Craft criteria. The first was Parker's 'Ko-Ko' (0005). I am noting this independently. Cluster C confirmation requires independent naming, and this is independent naming."

**Cluster C is confirmed.** Four reviewers, three of them at different weight levels and from different compositional traditions, named the improvised-Craft distinction independently at a second improvised work. The proposed amendment language (drafted at Work 0005) should now be ratified and incorporated into the Rubric as v1.2 language.

## 7. Cluster Analysis

**Cluster A: Structural Self-Proof** — UNCHANGED. Three confirmed works (0001, 0003, 0008). *A Love Supreme* is not a Cluster A work: the twelve-key transposition is a universality argument, not a self-proof. The motif does not prove something by being the demonstration of it; it argues something by being systematically completed in all its instances.

**Cluster B: Non-Developmental Architecture** — UNCHANGED. Two confirmed works (0002, 0004). *A Love Supreme* is a hybrid (DEVELOPMENTAL + MODAL) and introduces a potential new structural sub-type, but the twelve-key transposition is not the same as modal stasis (0002) or tintinnabuli stasis (0004).

**Cluster C: Improvised Music** — CONFIRMED. Two works: 0005 (Parker Ko-Ko) + 0009 (A Love Supreme, Part I). Four reviewers independently named the improvised-Craft distinction at the second work. Amendment ratification pending.

**Cluster D: Dramatic Music** — UNCHANGED. One work (0007). Still needs one additional musical-theater work to confirm.

**Cluster E: Synthesis Music** — UNCHANGED. One work (0010). Still needs one additional synthesis work to confirm.

**Cluster F (proposed): Devotional Music** — SEEDED. Innovation #19 (purpose-as-resonance-category) from this panel seeds a potential new cluster: works whose primary purpose is devotional offering rather than audience communication. *A Love Supreme* is the seed. Needs one additional devotional work to confirm. Candidates in the pipeline: Arvo Pärt's Passio (if added), John Tavener's Song for Athene, or similar.

## 8. Design Recommendations

**Recommendation 1: Ratify Cluster C amendment immediately.**
Four independent namings across two works from three different compositional traditions constitutes clear confirmation. The proposed Craft amendment language from Work 0005 should be incorporated into Rubric v1.2 as a permanent improvisation mode.

**Recommendation 2: Add Step 0 sub-type for universality-through-exhaustion.**
Draft language: *"Universality-through-Exhaustion: Structure built by completing a formal possibility in all its instances to demonstrate that the thing being named belongs to all instances simultaneously and is not constrained by any single instance. Assessment criterion: Is every instance present? Does the completeness constitute the structural argument? Does stopping short of all instances undermine the claim? Canonical instance: twelve-key transposition in A Love Supreme, Part I."*

**Recommendation 3: Draft Resonance purpose classification.**
Draft language: *"Before scoring Resonance, identify the work's primary purpose: (a) audience-communication — the intended effect is on the listener; score on whether the effect arrives; (b) interior-expression — the primary purpose is expressive regardless of listener reception; score on whether the expressive act is completely and honestly executed; (c) devotional offering — the primary purpose is directed toward something larger than the performance; score on whether the offering is genuine. For category (c), the listener's reception is a secondary effect; the primary Resonance question is the genuineness of the offering."*

**Score note.** 90.4 is within the expected range (88–92). The score reflects: Coltrane's self-assessment is honest and specific; Miles's two-point Resonance deduction for the non-religious limitation is legitimate and reflects real tradition-dependence in devotional music assessment; the unanimous Economy docking for the post-spoken-words closing section is the panel's most consistent finding and reflects a genuine structural economy question. The score is correct.

**Coltrane's Resonance note.** Coltrane is the first reviewer in the SCORE corpus to score a dimension at the ceiling (15/15 for Resonance) with the explicit statement that the score is not generosity but certainty — the certainty of a composer who knows what the offering was and knows it was genuine. This is the same epistemic position as Bach's structural self-proof: the proof either holds or it doesn't; the composer can verify this from the inside. Coltrane verifies the offering from the inside. The 15 is not self-flattery; it is the score that follows from certainty.
