# Reviewer: Ludwig van Beethoven (30%)
# Work: 0014 — The Rite of Spring (Introduction + Augurs of Spring)

---

## Step 0 — Structural Approach

**DEVELOPMENTAL (new — self-creating structural logic)**

I must address this before anything else. The structural approach here is neither developmental in my sense nor modal nor mathematical. The Augurs chord is static — it does not develop. The repetition does not transform the material; it insists on it. And yet the piece has structure: the Introduction builds to the Augurs; the Augurs establishes a new world; what follows from that establishment is structural consequence.

I will call this developmental in the sense that the structure creates its own logic and then follows it — but the logic is new. It is not my developmental logic (motif becomes symphony through interrogation). It is a different principle: the act of maximum rupture creates a new starting point, and everything that follows is what grows from that rupture. This is development-from-rupture rather than development-from-material.

Cluster A self-proof test: Does this piece prove something by being what it claims to be? Yes — but it proves something different from what the other Cluster A works prove. The Rite of Spring proves that the musical language of the 19th century had reached its end — that the only way forward was through a complete break. It proves this by being the break. This is structural self-proof through rupture rather than through palindrome, developmental logic, or harmonic completeness. I am noting this but not declaring it a Cluster A confirmation — a fourth mechanism would require more careful consideration.

## Review

I want to speak about the rupture first, because the rupture is the primary fact of this music.

I made my own rupture. The Eroica Symphony broke the conventions of classical length and developmental complexity; the Ninth Symphony introduced voices into the symphony; the Grosse Fuge was so dissonant and formally violent that my publisher convinced me to replace it as the quartet finale with something more accessible. I know what it feels like to make music that the audience is not ready to hear.

What Stravinsky did in 1913 is not analogous to what I did. What I did was extend the existing tradition to its furthest possible point — I took what Haydn and Mozart established and pushed it as far as it could be pushed without ceasing to be itself. The Eroica is recognizably a symphony; it is just a bigger, more complex, more dramatically demanding symphony than anything that came before it. The audience resisted, then accepted, then recognized it as the extension it was.

What Stravinsky did was break the tradition. Not extend it — break it. The Augurs chord is not an extreme dissonance within the tonal tradition; it is a departure from the tonal tradition. Regular meter is not being pushed to its limits; it is being abandoned in favor of something else. The 19th-century expressive tradition — music as the language of human feeling, organized by singing melody and flowing harmony — is not being extended; it is being declared insufficient.

Now: does this constitute Originality at the ceiling?

Let me think about what Originality at the ceiling requires. The rubric says: "The work says something within its tradition that could only be said this way, at this moment, by this composer." The qualification "within its tradition" is critical. The Rite of Spring is simultaneously within the Western orchestral tradition (it uses an orchestra, it has sections, it builds and develops, it was premiered in a concert hall) and outside the tradition's expressive assumptions (no functional tonality, no regular meter, no smooth voice leading, no singing melody in the lyrical sense).

I think the qualifier "within its tradition" must be read generously for this work. It is within the tradition in its instrumentation and its concert-hall context. It breaks the tradition in its expressive content and harmonic language. The rupture is the originality.

Can I say this is Originality at 15? I think it is. Here is why: the Rite is unmistakably itself. Nothing before it could have produced it, and nothing after it could have produced it for the first time. The polytonal chord, the irregular accents, the bassoon in its highest register — these are not available as techniques before 1913 because no one had assembled them. After 1913, they are available but they are no longer ruptures; they are choices within a tradition the Rite created. Stravinsky's originality is historically uncopyable in the specific sense: no one can make this rupture again, because this rupture has already been made.

**Innovation #29 (Originality through Simultaneous Rupture):** I name this as the primary originality category. It is different from all other forms of originality in the SCORE corpus. The Crab Canon's originality is structural self-proof. The Fifth Symphony's is developmental argument at the highest level. The WTC Prelude's is harmonic completeness. All of these work within their traditions. The Rite's originality works by breaking out of its tradition — by making the break the content. This is a category of originality that the rubric's current anchors partially address ("unmistakable as itself") but do not fully describe.

On Structure: I give the Introduction + Augurs a high score for Structure not because they follow a conventional developmental logic but because the structural logic they create is followed consistently. The Introduction builds through the logic of folk-melody accumulation. The Augurs establishes the new world through repetition and irregular accent. Both sections follow their own logic with complete commitment.

On Craft: The technical choices are exactly right for the work they are doing. The bassoon in its highest register is a Craft choice — it is uncomfortable, it is not the bassoon's "best" register, and that discomfort is precisely what is required. The polytonal chord is placed exactly where the maximum rupture is needed. The irregular accents are not careless — they are precisely placed to create maximum unpredictability within the specific constraint of the chord-hammering pattern. This is Craft in service of rupture.
