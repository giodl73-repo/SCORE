# Work: 0014 — The Rite of Spring (Introduction + Augurs of Spring)

## Step 0 — Structural Approach

**DEVELOPMENTAL (new — self-creating structural logic)**

The structural approach requires a new sub-type. Neither the existing Step 0 categories nor the standard developmental category captures what the Rite of Spring does. I propose: **developmental through maximum rupture** — a structural approach in which the architecture is built not through gradual transformation of material (standard development) but through the establishment of a rupture point from which everything else proceeds as structural consequence. The Introduction sets up; the Augurs chord breaks. Everything after the Augurs chord is a consequence of having broken.

## Review

I will work through the theoretical content systematically.

**Polytonality: The Augurs Chord**

The chord is analytically described as an Eb dominant seventh (Eb-G-Bb-Db) superimposed on an E major triad (E-G#-B), played simultaneously. The theoretical name is a polytonal superimposition — two complete harmonic objects in different keys, coexisting.

The specific choice of E and Eb is maximally dissonant: the two key centers are a semitone apart, meaning that the pitch content of one key conflicts maximally with the pitch content of the other. This is not accidentally dissonant; it is systematically dissonant — the most dissonant polytonal superimposition available in the twelve-tone chromatic system.

Within functional tonal harmony, this chord has no resolution. There is no next chord to which this chord naturally moves. The tonal system requires resolution; this chord denies it. The harmonic consequence: the listener's expectation of resolution is perpetually denied throughout the Augurs section, creating continuous tension without release.

**Polyrhythm and Metric Irregularity**

The accent pattern in the Augurs of Spring is analytically specified in the score. The accents do not follow a regular metric pattern; they create irregular groupings that change from bar to bar. This is not improvised irregularity; it is composed irregularity. The pattern is: each accent placement is specified exactly in the score, and the pattern of placements changes with each repetition.

The theoretical precedent for this kind of irregular accentuation is limited before 1913. Folk-music traditions (including some of the Eastern European folk sources Stravinsky was drawing from) use irregular rhythmic groupings; classical music uses irregular rhythms as occasional exceptions (hemiola, syncopation). Using systematic irregular accent as the primary rhythmic language of an extended orchestral section is new in the Western classical tradition.

**The Bassoon Opening: Register as Harmonic Choice**

The bassoon's highest register has a specific acoustic property: the instrument's harmonics at this pitch level produce a more complex, less stable tone color than in its comfortable lower register. This is not merely a timbral choice; it is a harmonic choice. The upper-register bassoon tone color contains more partial tones in dissonant relationships than the lower-register tone. The opening melody is harmonically complex in its timbre even before any other instrument enters.

**Analytical Assessment**

Within the harmonic language the work establishes, the technical choices are internally consistent. The polytonality is maintained throughout the Augurs section without resolution. The irregular accents are precisely specified. The orchestration (as developed into the full score) is analytically coherent: instruments enter in combinations that reinforce rather than obscure the polytonal clash.

The voice leading question: within the piece's own premises (polytonal superimposition as the primary harmonic unit), the voice leading is correct. Individual voices move by step or by small intervals; the polytonal dissonance is a product of the relationship between layers, not of careless individual voice movement.

**The New Structural Category**

I formalize what other reviewers will name: this piece requires a new Step 0 sub-type. "Developmental through rupture" — a structural approach in which maximum disruption of prior convention creates a new starting point, and the architecture is what grows from that starting point. Not the same as Developmental (gradual transformation of material), not Modal/Static (sustained tonal gravity), not Mathematical/Formal (formal relationship), not Minimalist/Additive (gradual change), not Universality-through-Exhaustion (systematic completion). The rupture creates the form.
