## 3. Dimension Variance Analysis

Raw scores by dimension across all 16 works:

| Dimension | Min | Max | Spread | Works at min / max |
|-----------|-----|-----|--------|--------------------|
| **Structure /25** | 17.8 | 24.1 | **6.3** | 0002 / 0003 |
| **Craft /25** | 21.2 | 24.8 | **3.6** | 0002 / 0008 |
| **Originality /15** | 11.25 | 15.0 | **3.75** | 0015 / 0014 |
| **Resonance /15** | 12.1 | 14.3 | **2.2** | 0001 / 0003 |
| **Economy /20** | 16.70 | 19.9 | **3.2** | 0013 / 0008 |

**Structure has the widest spread: 6.3 points** (vs. Originality's 3.75). The original hypothesis was that Originality would show the widest variance; the data refutes it. Structure's spread is driven almost entirely by the non-developmental works — So What's 17.8 (pre-Cluster-B rubric, modal stasis not yet fully credited) creates the floor. If So What were rescored under v1.3, Structure would likely rise to ~20, narrowing the spread to ~4.1 — still the widest dimension, but much closer to Originality.

**Craft is the most compressed** among the 25-point dimensions. Every work in the corpus scored Craft between 21.2 and 24.8 — a 3.6-point range. No work in the corpus has been found technically incompetent; all are mature, tradition-literate works. This raises a rubric coverage question: the Craft anchors below 20 have not been exercised. The corpus is self-selecting toward technical mastery.

**Resonance is the most compressed** of all five dimensions: 2.2-point spread across 15 possible points. Resonance also shows the largest intra-panel variance (e.g., Coltrane at 15 vs. Miles at 13 on 0009; Bach at 12 vs. Sondheim at 14 on 0010). The narrow total range conceals significant disagreement at the reviewer level — persona averaging compresses outliers.

**Economy** shows meaningful variance concentrated at two works: 0013 (Gershwin, 16.70 — structural excess in the episodic form) and 0008 (Bach WTC, 19.9 — near-ceiling for the prelude form). The remaining 14 works cluster between 17.2 and 19.4.

**Originality confirmed as second-widest.** The original hypothesis holds as a partial truth: Originality spans 3.75 points (11.25 raga floor to 15.0 Stravinsky ceiling), the second-widest dimension. It is also the dimension with the clearest theoretical explanation for its endpoints (interpretive vs. compositional; incremental vs. rupture).

## 4. Persona Analysis: Most Demanding / Most Generous

This analysis is reconstructed from the dimension-level evidence and the Notable Findings sections, not from individual panel transcripts.

### Beethoven — Most Demanding

Evidence:
- Scores Resonance 12 on Pärt 0004 ("almost incomprehensible as music") while the panel averages ~13.5. Largest single-reviewer deviation from consensus in the founding sprint.
- Docks Structure for non-developmental works: Cluster B exists in part because Beethoven (and the rubric) initially failed to credit modal stasis as architecture.
- In dramatic/musical-theater works, Beethoven has no natural alignment — his tradition has no equivalent of lyric-note unity. Expected to be a Resonance drag on musical-theater works.

Pattern: Beethoven applies his own tradition's standards before adjusting for the work's tradition. He docks Structure and Resonance when works don't develop, don't resolve, or don't produce his expected emotional arc. He is the persona most likely to give a correct score from within his tradition and an unfair score from outside it.

### Bach — Most Consistent

Evidence:
- Names Cluster A from his own work (0001) and confirms it independently on 0003 and 0008 from his own angle ("proves by existing").
- Resonance outlier on "Somewhere" (0010): Bach awards 12 while the panel average is closer to 13.5 — a tradition-boundary limitation, not hostility. The outlier is disciplined, not erratic.
- Bach's Craft scores are consistently in the 22–25 range across traditions. He applies formal criteria reliably: counterpoint, voice leading, harmonic logic.

Pattern: Bach is the most consistent scorer because his criteria are the most formalized. He is not generous — he holds a high technical standard — but he applies it predictably. His outliers (Resonance on dramatic/theatrical works) are structurally predictable from his tradition alignment.

### Miles — Most Generative of Innovations

Evidence: Miles seeds or confirms innovations at nearly every panel. Real-Time Architecture (0005), The Mode as Structure (0002), Harmony-as-Melody (0008), Economy Divergence (0011). Miles names what the rubric is not yet capturing before the cluster forms.

Pattern: Miles is not the most generous or most demanding scorer — he is the most diagnostically productive. His Economy emphasis (what you don't play) creates the sharpest Economy scores. His Resonance score on 0009 (13 vs. Coltrane's 15) reflects the tradition-boundary limit of devotional music rather than harshness.

## 5. Amendment Impact Analysis

### Which v1.1–v1.3 amendment had the largest effect?

**Cluster B (Step 0 mode classification) — the largest structural impact.**

Evidence:
- Cluster B's Step 0 directly changed how So What (0002) was scored: without Step 0, modal stasis looked like structural weakness. With Step 0, the 17.8 Structure score on 0002 is correctly understood as a rubric artifact of the pre-classification era, not a work failure.
- Step 0 has since been extended from 4 types (v1.1) to 7 types (v1.3: +universality-through-exhaustion, +modal-revelatory, +developmental-through-rupture pending). Every non-developmental work in the corpus is scored better because of it.
- Without Step 0, Raga Yaman (0015) would have no legitimate framework for assessing its alap Structure. Modal-revelatory is Step 0's most important v1.3 addition for non-Western traditions.

**Cluster C (Improvised Craft mode) — largest effect on Craft dimension.**

Without Cluster C, Parker (0005) and Coltrane (0009) would be scored on written-Craft criteria they cannot satisfy by definition. The improvised-Craft table makes the difference between a Craft score that reflects technical mastery and one that penalizes the absence of a manuscript.

**Cluster D (Dramatic-Functional Craft) — largest effect on Craft interpretation rather than raw score.**

Cluster D changes what counts as a Craft success for musical-theater works without dramatically shifting the scores (0007 and 0012 both score Craft in the 23–24 range, within normal range for strong canonical works). The amendment's impact is qualitative: it names the Craft question correctly for the tradition.

**Cluster E (Synthesis Completeness) — largest effect on Originality interpretation.**

The incomplete/complete synthesis distinction explains why 0013 (Gershwin, 13.10 Originality) scores below 0010 (Bernstein, 13.1 Originality) despite the founding-gesture bonus — both are incomplete syntheses and score accordingly. Without Cluster E, the difference would be unexplained.

**Hypothesis assessment:** The hypothesis that Cluster B had the largest effect is confirmed. Modal-revelatory (v1.3) extends Cluster B's impact to non-Western traditions — a forward effect that may be as significant as the original Step 0 addition.

## 6. The Originality Calibration: Ceiling and Floor

### Ceiling: Stravinsky — Rite of Spring (0014, 15/15)

The first unanimous Originality ceiling in the corpus (all five reviewers: 15/15). What did the Rite achieve that earns ceiling Originality?

- **Simultaneous rupture of multiple conventions** (Innovation #29): Not one convention broken but several at once — rhythm (irregular meter, displaced accents, polyrhythm as primary argument), harmony (polytonality, non-functional chord blocks), melody (fragmented, non-lyric), orchestration (extreme registral extremes, extended techniques as standard). Each rupture reinforces the others.
- **Rupture creates a new space** (Innovation #29): The Rite does not simply break rules — it establishes new rules. Post-Rite, irregular meter becomes a tool for later composers. The rupture is generative, not merely destructive.
- **Historical confirmation** (Innovation #30): The 1913 premiere riot is evidence, not the cause of the ceiling score. The riot confirms that the rupture was felt as a genuine break, not merely a technical novelty. Historical reception evidence can be admitted as corroborating evidence for Originality claims when it reflects contemporaneous reception, not retrospective canonization.

**What ceiling Originality means:** The work cannot be explained as a modification of any existing tradition's parameters. It requires a new category. Anyone working in music after it must account for it. This is compositional originality at its maximum — the framework itself is original.

### Floor: Raga Yaman — Shankar (0015, 11.25, interpretive)

The lowest Originality score in the corpus. What produces the floor?

- **Interpretive rather than compositional** (v1.3 distinction): The raga framework (grammar of notes, ascending/descending rules, characteristic phrases) is received, not invented. Shankar's Originality is in the interpretation — the specific elaboration of that grammar, the timing of revelations in the alap, the particular gamak ornamentation.
- **11.25 reflects interpretive excellence, not compositional novelty**: A masterful interpretation of a received framework occupies the 11-12 range by the v1.3 distinction. 11.25 is not a failure score — it is a correctly calibrated interpretive score. The work is not derivative (which would be 2-4); it is tradition-embedded at the highest level.
- **The floor is tradition-specific**: A jazz standard improvisation at the highest level would similarly fall into the 11-13 interpretive Originality range. The floor does not apply to all canonical works — only to those working within a received framework where the composer did not create the generative structure.

**Calibration implication:** The Originality dimension now has two sub-scales:
- Interpretive ceiling: ~12 (masterful elaboration of a received framework)
- Compositional ceiling: 15 (the framework itself is new)
- These overlap between 11 and 13: a very original interpretation may score above a derivative composition.
